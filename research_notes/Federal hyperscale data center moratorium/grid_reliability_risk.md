# Data Center Load Growth and US Grid Reliability Risk (as of October 2, 2026)

Method note: research was done Oct 2, 2026. Direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy for most domains (nerc.com, utilitydive.com, pjm.com, texasre.org, law-firm sites), so findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages. Figures should be checked against the primary PDFs linked where possible. Where only secondary or aggregator sources were available, this is noted.

## 1. NERC Long-Term, Summer and Winter Reliability Assessments: which regions are at risk and how much is due to data centers

### Takeaway
NERC's assessments have moved steadily toward alarm. The 2024 LTRA (Dec 2024) put more than half of North America at elevated or high risk. The 2025 LTRA, released around Jan 29, 2026, rates 13 of 23 assessment areas as elevated or high risk within five years. MISO, PJM, ERCOT, SERC-Central, WECC-Northwest and WECC-Basin are rated high risk, and data centers and AI are named as the main driver of a 224 GW 10-year jump in peak demand. The seasonal assessments for 2025-26 are calmer. Normal peaks are covered, and the danger is concentrated in extreme weather and in later years.

### Cited Findings
- **2024 LTRA (Dec 17, 2024):** "Well over half of the continent is at elevated or high risk of energy shortfalls over the next 5 to 10 years." MISO was initially rated high risk, the worst ranking of any region. — [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/electric-utility-operations/news/55250881/nercs-2024-ltra-highlights-growing-energy-demand-and-critical-challenges-for-grid-reliability-over-the-next-decade); [NERC 2024 LTRA PDF](https://nerc.com/pa/RAPA/ra/Reliability%20Assessments%20DL/NERC_Long%20Term%20Reliability%20Assessment_2024.pdf)
- **Correction to 2024 LTRA:** MISO had submitted mismatched data that overstated the near-term shortfall. With corrected data, MISO was reclassified as "elevated" in the near term, becoming "high risk in the 2028–2031 timeframe." — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/nerc-miso-reliability-market-monitor-ferc-capacity/750037/)
- **2024 LTRA on data centers:** emerging large loads such as data centers pose "unique challenges to forecasting and planning." Customer-initiated automatic disconnection of large data-center load during normal grid faults was flagged as an "emerging concern." — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/nerc-tees-plan-assess-grid-risks-associated-data-centers); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/explosive-demand-growth-blackouts-NERC-LTRA-reliability/735866/)
- **2025 LTRA:** summer peak demand is forecast to grow 224 GW over 10 years, which is more than 69% above the previous year's forecast. Data centers and AI are the cited driver. — [POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/nerc-warns-long-term-grid-reliability-risks-mounting/); [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/business/policy-and-regulation/nerc-warns-reliability-risk-is-rising-as-load-growth-outpaces-infrastructure/)
- **2025 LTRA high-risk (red) areas:** MISO, PJM, Texas RE-ERCOT, WECC-Northwest, WECC-Basin and SERC-Central. In these areas, planned resources would produce energy shortfalls beyond adequacy targets. 13 of 23 assessment areas are at elevated or high risk over the next five years. NERC defines "high risk" as falling short of a regulator's reserve-margin target, or exceeding unserved-energy and loss-of-load criteria that roughly match a 1-day-in-10-years standard. — [POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/nerc-warns-long-term-grid-reliability-risks-mounting/); [Microgrid Knowledge](https://www.microgridknowledge.com/grid/article/55355943/nerc-warns-of-2028-capacity-shortfalls-amid-exploding-ai-and-data-center-loads)
- **Release date:** search summaries give a release date of January 29, 2026 for the "2025" LTRA, not December 2025. The exact date has not been confirmed from NERC directly. — [Microgrid Knowledge](https://www.microgridknowledge.com/grid/article/55355943/nerc-warns-of-2028-capacity-shortfalls-amid-exploding-ai-and-data-center-loads)
- **Independent review:** Grid Strategies published a critique of the 2025 LTRA. Its contents could not be fetched. — [Grid Strategies PDF](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/wp-content/uploads/FINAL-2025-LTRA-Review.pdf)
- **2025-26 Winter Reliability Assessment (Nov 2025):**
  - Demand has grown about 20 GW since the previous winter, while winter capacity grew by less than half that amount.
  - Winter demand growth is fastest in data-center-heavy areas.
  - Resources are adequate for a normal peak, but "much of North America" is at risk in extreme, prolonged, wide-area cold.
  - NERC's director of reliability assessments warned that in extreme winter conditions, actual demand can exceed forecasts by as much as 25%.
  — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/data-centers-drive-elevated-risk-of-electricity-shortages-this-winter); [EUCI](https://www.euci.com/electric-grids-set-to-weather-the-winter-data-center-demands-may-pose-a-challenge/)
- **2026 Summer Reliability Assessment (May 19, 2026):** only 3 areas are at elevated risk: New England, the Pacific Northwest and Saskatchewan. That is down from 6 the year before, helped by a record of about 58 GW of additions. In none of those three areas is the cause data centers: New England's is reduced firm imports, and the Pacific Northwest's is drought-reduced hydro. NERC said data-center demand "could erode resource margins below targets later in the decade" in ERCOT and PJM. — [GridBeyond](https://gridbeyond.com/us-to-have-adequate-resources-to-meet-normal-summer-peak-demand-says-nerc/); [mgrid.org](https://mgrid.org/?p=9499); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/renewables/1797462/the-power-grid-passed-its-report-card-but-read-the-fine-print)
- **Regional data-center loads in NERC projections:**
  - MISO summer peak rises from 127 GW (2026) to 143.7 GW (2035), including 18 GW of data centers.
  - ERCOT summer peak rises from 94.7 GW to 154.1 GW, including 23 GW of data centers.
  - PJM rises by about 56 GW in summer and 62 GW in winter by the mid-2030s.
  — [mgrid.org summary of NERC](https://mgrid.org/?p=9499)

### Inferences
- NERC's resource-adequacy warnings are regional and forward-looking (2027–2031). They are not about an imminent nationwide shortfall. Near-term seasonal risk mostly reflects weather and imports, not data centers.
- The risk concentrates in organized markets with heavy data-center queues: PJM, ERCOT and MISO, plus SERC-Central.

### Gaps
- I could not access the 2025 LTRA PDF to quantify what share of the 224 GW NERC attributes specifically to data centers.
- The Dec 2025 vs Jan 2026 release date is unconfirmed.
- The 2026-27 Winter Reliability Assessment would normally come out in Nov 2026, so it is not yet published.

## 2. DOE July 2025 Resource Adequacy Report ("100x blackout risk")

### Takeaway
DOE's July 7, 2025 report, ordered by a Trump executive order, claims loss-of-load hours could rise about 100-fold by 2030. That figure comes from a scenario with 104 GW of retirements and essentially no new resources after 2026 beyond those already planned. Critics, including GridLab and clean-energy groups, say this assumption, plus undercounting of wind, solar and storage, drives the result.

### Cited Findings
- DOE released the report on July 7, 2025 under Executive Order 14262 on grid reliability. DOE warned that blackouts could increase 100x in 2030 if firm capacity keeps retiring without enough replacement. — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/node/4851683); [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/reliability-and-resiliency/news/55301722/department-of-energy-releases-report-on-us-grid-reliability-and-security)
- **The scenario:** 104 GW of retirements are replaced by 209 GW of new generation by 2030, of which only 22 GW is firm. Loss-of-load hours rise from 8.1 per year to 817 per year in the plant-closures case. Even with no retirements, outage risk in several regions rises more than 30-fold. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/load-growth-plant-retirements-blackouts-doe/752408/); [GridLab](https://gridlab.org/gridlab-analysis-department-of-energy-resource-adequacy-report/)
- **Method:** hourly simulation of 23 US subregions with interregional transfer limits, using 12 historical weather years. — [Energy Institute New Energy World](https://knowledge.energyinst.org/new-energy-world/article?id=139737)
- **GridLab's critique:**
  - The report uses aggressive load-growth and retirement assumptions but conservative supply assumptions.
  - It effectively assumes no new resources after 2026, even though utilities and markets have plans and queues.
  - It "undercounts the resources likely to be added... and overstates expected retirements."
  — [GridLab](https://gridlab.org/gridlab-analysis-department-of-energy-resource-adequacy-report/)
- **Other critics:**
  - Clean-energy groups said it "appears to exaggerate the risk of blackouts and undervalue the contributions of entire resource classes."
  - They said it lacked public input, even though the EO directs it to be used to identify plants to keep online.
  - Sierra Club called it a "false narrative."
  — [Energy Institute](https://knowledge.energyinst.org/new-energy-world/article?id=139737); [Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/clean-energy-groups-challenge-doe-grid-reliability-report/757912); [Advanced Energy United](https://advancedenergyunited.org/blog/doe-resource-adequacy-report/)
- The report has been used to justify DOE 202(c) emergency orders keeping plants online. Washington State filed for rehearing and a stay of one such order. — [DOE docket](https://www.energy.gov/documents/motion-intervene-request-rehearing-and-motion-stay-state-washington-202-26-28-exhibits-0)

### Inferences
- The "100x" headline is a stress-test result driven by its assumptions, not a forecast. Treat it as an upper-bound advocacy figure. The no-retirement 30x result is a more defensible signal, but it still assumes no additions after 2026.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed or NERC/RTO-endorsed replication of DOE's numbers.

## 3. PJM capacity auctions, ERCOT, MISO, SPP and the Southeast

### Takeaway
PJM is the clearest case where data centers are causing measurable resource-adequacy shortfalls.

- **Prices:** capacity prices jumped about 10x, from $28.92/MW-day (2024/25) to $269.92 (2025/26). The last three auctions (2026/27, 2027/28, 2028/29) all cleared at the FERC-approved cap of roughly $325–333/MW-day.
- **Shortfalls:** the last two cleared about 6.6–6.8 GW short of the reliability requirement, the first such shortfalls in PJM's history.
- **Cause:** nearly all forecast load growth is from data centers.

ERCOT faces enormous speculative large-load queues and has adopted a heavily discounted forecast.

### Cited Findings
- **2025/26 BRA:** $269.92/MW-day, reserve margin 18.5%. **2026/27 BRA** (held July 2025): $329.17/MW-day at the cap, reserve margin 18.9% vs a 19.1% target. — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2025/7/pjm-capacity-auction-prices-clear-at-record-highs-for-2nd-year-in-a-row-91640293); [NUS Consulting](https://www.nusconsulting.com/energy-news/pjm-capacity-auction-2026-2027/)
- **2027/28 BRA** (Dec 2025):
  - Cleared at the $333.44/MW-day cap.
  - Short of the 1-in-10 reliability requirement by 6,623 MW UCAP, giving a reserve margin of 14.8% vs a target of about 20%.
  - This was the first time the whole RTO, including FRR areas, fell short.
  - About 5,100 MW of the 5,250 MW peak-forecast increase is attributed to data centers.
  — [Power Engineering / Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/pjm-capacity-auction-hits-price-cap-again-as-region-falls-short-of-reliability-target); [NRDC](https://www.nrdc.org/press-releases/first-time-history-pjm-auction-fails-procure-necessary-power-supply)
- **2028/29 BRA** (results July 14, 2026):
  - Procured 138,318 MW.
  - Cleared at the $325/MW-day cap in all zones, the third straight auction at the collar.
  - Fell 6,831 MW short.
  - The uncapped price would have been about $554.72.
  - Only about 525 MW of genuinely new generation cleared.
  — [PJM press release](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2026-releases/20260714-pjm-capacity-auction-procures-138318-mw-of-generation-resources.pdf); [PJM results report](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/markets-ops/rpm/rpm-auction-info/2028-2029/2028-2029-bra-results-report.pdf); [Enel](https://www.enelnorthamerica.com/insights/blogs/pjm-2028-2029-capacity-auction-results)
- **Backstop procurement:** PJM filed a one-time Reliability Backstop Procurement at FERC on July 31, 2026, seeking 6.8 GW. The auction window is Sept 30–Oct 21, 2026, with 15-year commitments and a $555/MW-day cap, intended to make data centers bear their share. Status at Oct 2, 2026: the window has just opened, and the outcome is unknown. — [New Project Media](https://newprojectmedia.com/auction-pjm-auction-hits-price-cap-clears-6-8-gw-short-of-reliability-requirement/); [Oilprice.com](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/PJM-Auction-Comes-Up-68-Gigawatts-Short-As-Data-Centers-Devour-Power.amp.html)
- **ERCOT large-load queue:**
  - Large-load demand under study nearly tripled in about 11 months, from about 72 GW to over 200 GW. Data centers and crypto account for more than 80%.
  - ERCOT's protocol-based long-term forecast (April 2026) produced about 368 GW, roughly 4x today's peak. ERCOT itself warned this is likely overinflated.
  - On June 18, 2026, the PUCT directed ERCOT to base its adjusted forecast on the much smaller "Batch Zero" base load.
  — [ERCOT CEO update, June 2026](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/06/01/11-CEO-Update-REVISED.pdf); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/texas-demand-to-quadruple-by-2032-ercot-says-maybe-but-dont-bet-on-it/817698/); [Community Impact](https://beta2.communityimpact.com/austin/central-austin/government/2026/04/22/ercot-forecasts-quadruple-growth-in-electric-demand-warns-estimate-is-likely-overinflated/)
- **Texas SB 6 (2025):** the PUCT is implementing large-load interconnection rules (curtailment and ride-through obligations, cost allocation). ERCOT's Large Load Working Group is addressing ride-through. — [NASEO/PUCT SB6 presentation, Oct 2025](https://mojo.naseo.org/Data/Sites/1/media/tknaseo/sb6-puct-17-oct-2025.pdf); [Texas RE Nov 2025](https://texasre.org/pages/newsletters/november2025/largeloadupdate)
- **MISO:** rated high risk in the 2025 LTRA. NERC projects 18 GW of data centers by 2035. — [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/nerc-warns-long-term-grid-reliability-risks-mounting/); [mgrid.org](https://mgrid.org/?p=9499)

### Inferences
- PJM shows the most concrete evidence that data-center load is outrunning supply: two consecutive auctions below the reliability requirement, with data centers about 97% of forecast growth in 2027/28. Even so, a 14.8% reserve margin is a planning-criterion miss (falling below the 1-in-10 standard), not a forecast of blackouts. Actual outages depend on weather, demand response and whether forecast data-center load actually materializes.
- In ERCOT, forecast inflation from speculative queue entries is itself acknowledged by the operator.

### Gaps
- Not researched in depth because of the tool-call budget: SPP and Southeast (Georgia Power, Duke, TVA) specifics. SERC-Central is high risk per the 2025 LTRA, but I did not find the drivers.
- Outcome of PJM's backstop auction: pending as of Oct 2, 2026.

## 4. Data-center-specific technical failure mechanisms and incidents (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
This is the most novel and best-documented risk. A series of events has repeatedly shown the same failure mode. Fault-sensitive data-center protection systems transfer gigawatts of load to on-site backup power within seconds after ordinary, cleared transmission faults. Those events have grown in size: 1,500 MW (July 2024), 1,800 MW (Feb 2025), 1,300 MW (June 2025), and about 3,800–4,000 MW (July 22, 2026).

So far none has caused customer outages or bulk-system instability, but they produced measurable frequency excursions. NERC escalated to a rare Level 3 alert (May 4, 2026), and FERC ordered mandatory standards (July 16, 2026). AI-training power oscillations are a second, emerging mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- **July 10, 2024 (Northern Virginia, Dominion/PJM):**
  - A 230 kV line fault led to about 1,500 MW of data-center-only load transferring to backup across about 60 points and 25 substations.
  - The load was dropped on the customer side by data-center protection and controls, not by utility equipment.
  - Frequency rose sharply, then voltages rose, and operators had to intervene.
  - NERC published its incident review on Jan 8, 2025.
  — [TRC Companies](https://www.trccompanies.com/insights/large-data-center-load-loss-raises-for-system-impact-studies); [NERC presentation at FERC, Apr 2025](https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2025-04/Presentation%20NERC%20Seeks%20to%20Address%20Reliability%20Impacts%20from%C2%A0Large%20Load%20Integration_1.pdf)
- **2025 events in NERC's 2026 State of Reliability:**
  - Feb 2025: about 1,800 MW lost in Loudoun and Fairfax counties after a transmission fault.
  - June 2025: about 1,300 MW.
  - ERCOT had nine crypto-mining load-loss events above 100 MW in 2025.
  — [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/nerc-flags-ai-data-center-grid-risks-in-report); [APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/bulk-power-system-continued-perform-reliably-2025-new-and-emerging-risks-are-evolving-nerc)
- **April 18, 2026:** a fault in the same Northern Virginia vicinity, with characteristics similar to July 2024, led to only about 500 MW of sustained load loss. This suggests some mitigation improvement. — [NERC Reliability Insights, Sept 2026 (PDF, via search summary)](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/who-we-are/news/2026/09/reliability-insights_large-computational-load-risks-due-to-voltage-sensitivity.pdf)
- **July 22, 2026 (largest to date):**
  - A 230 kV fault in the Dominion zone at 07:56 caused about 2,900 MW of data-center load to transfer to backup, followed by about 1,000 MW about a minute later. That is nearly 4,000 MW in total (reported as about 3,800 MW), about 3% of PJM load.
  - Frequency reached 60.092 Hz, and reporting ACE reached +3,928 MW.
  - PJM said there was no bulk-system reliability impact and no load shed. Dominion said the data centers' own protection systems initiated the transfer.
  - PJM later said the facilities "tripped too early" after a cleared fault.
  - PJM is developing emergency procedures for gigawatt-scale loads and large-load ride-through requirements (Planning Committee, Sept 15, 2026).
  — [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/fault-in-data-center-alley-triggered-3-gw-load-drop-on-pjm); [PJM Inside Lines](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-dominion-review-large-load-transfer-event/); [PJM OC presentation, Aug 6, 2026](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/committees/oc/2026/20260806/20260806-item-05---dominion-july-loss-of-load-event---presentation.pdf); [PJM SOS presentation, July 30, 2026](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/subcommittees/sos/2026/20260730/20260730-item-06---july-22-dominion-load-transfer-event---presentation.pdf); [PJM LCL ride-through presentation](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/committees/pc/2026/20260915/20260915-item-02---lcl-ride-through-requirements-background-education---presentation.pdf); [mgrid.org](https://mgrid.org/?p=15942)
- **NERC Level 3 Essential Action alert (May 4, 2026):**
  - Covers computational-load modeling, studies, instrumentation, commissioning, operations, protection and control.
  - Seven essential actions, with responses due Aug 3, 2026.
  - Applies to transmission planners and operators, planning coordinators and balancing authorities.
  - Prompted by a pattern of 1,000+ MW of computational load dropping off "in seconds," described as comparable to a large generator trip.
  - Reported as only the third Level 3 alert in NERC history.
  — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/energy--environmental-law-blog/2026/05/nerc-level-3-alert-large-loads-data-centers); [Carbon Direct](https://www.carbon-direct.com/insights/nerc-level-3-alert-data-center-loads); [Utility Dive via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/sudden-data-center-load-losses-072609520.html); [eePower](https://eepower.com/news/nerc-warns-ai-data-centers-threaten-grid-reliability/)
- **Modeling gap:** NERC reportedly found operators cannot reliably model how 77% of currently operating data-center load behaves during disturbances. This comes from secondary sources and was not verified in a NERC document. — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/officials-issue-their-highest-alert-yet-on-data-centers-electricity-demand/); [APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/node/31188)
- **NERC on scaling risk:** "As the size of individual large load facilities increases and if large load facilities are located near one another, the sizes of these large load events will likely increase," potentially leading "to measurable frequency or voltage stability issues." — [NERC RSTC whitepaper, Characteristics and Risks of Emerging Large Loads](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/who-we-are/standing-committees/rstc/whitepaper-characteristics-and-risks-of-emerging-large-loads.pdf)
- **FERC order (July 16, 2026):**
  - Directs NERC to file mandatory Reliability Standards for "computational loads" by Dec 31, 2026, plus Glossary and Rules of Procedure changes, with an informational filing due March 1, 2027.
  - Computational loads are currently not subject to NERC standards and are not registered entities.
  - FERC cited NERC-documented disturbances in which computational loads caused or contributed to instability.
  — [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutmanenergyreport.com/2026/07/ferc-directs-nerc-to-submit-rules-addressing-risks-associated-with-integration-of-computational-loads-into-bulk-power-system/); [FERC order PDF hosted by NERC](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/initiatives/large-loads-action-plan/ferc-order-on-computational-loads.pdf); [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/ferc-orders-mandatory-nerc-reliability-standards-for-data-center-and-other-computational-loads/); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/ferc-sets-speedy-timeline-to-address-data-centers-risks-to-grid-reliability/)
- **AI-training power oscillations:** NERC treats rapid, large, synchronized power swings from AI training clusters as an emerging risk. Recent academic work proposes active filters and hybrid storage to buffer datacenter-scale training fluctuations, and analyzes modal (oscillation) interactions in data-center-dominated systems. All of these are preprints, not incident evidence. — [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55376679/why-nerc-now-sees-ai-data-centers-as-grid-actors); [arXiv 2609.13490](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13490); [arXiv 2606.04869](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04869); [arXiv 2606.13847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13847); [arXiv 2609.03030 (ride-through hosting capacity)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03030)

### Inferences
- **How the failure works:** a normal transmission fault causes a voltage sag. Clustered data-center UPS and protection systems, all set to similar tight thresholds, transfer to backup simultaneously. The grid then suddenly loses gigawatts of load, which causes over-frequency and overvoltage. This is a common-mode failure that conventional planning (built around losing generation, not load) did not cover. The worst case is a loss of load at or beyond the largest single-contingency design basis, coinciding with other stress, or oscillatory interactions. That could in principle trip generators or cause voltage instability. No event so far has done so.
- **Outcome so far:** the incidents were absorbed. Even the about 4 GW event moved Eastern Interconnection frequency only to about 60.09 Hz. The large inertia of the Eastern Interconnection is a protective factor. ERCOT, which is smaller and has lower inertia, would be more sensitive to an equal-MW event.
- **Regulatory trend:** the response is engineering fixes (ride-through standards, modeling, registration), not load limits.

### Gaps
- Could not read the NERC Sept 2026 Reliability Insights PDF in full.
- No confirmed incident of AI-training oscillations causing a grid event was found.
- No documented customer outage caused by data-center load behavior was found.

## 6. Is a "nationwide grid collapse" technically plausible?

### Takeaway
Not from data-center load growth in any documented mechanism. The contiguous US runs as three asynchronous AC interconnections (Eastern, Western and ERCOT/Texas), linked only by limited-capacity HVDC ties. Those ties do not carry fault currents or cascade AC instability across the boundary. Realistic failure modes from data-center growth are:

1. Regional resource shortfalls that lead to controlled rotating outages during extreme weather, in the style of ERCOT in Feb 2021.
2. Local or regional instability from large simultaneous load loss within one interconnection.

The "nine substations black out the whole US" claim (FERC's Wellinghoff, 2014) concerns coordinated physical attack, not load growth. It came from an unpublished FERC analysis and is not an engineering consensus.

### Cited Findings
- The continental US has three power grids (Western, Eastern, Texas) interconnected only by high-voltage DC ties. — [Overbye, Texas A&M](https://overbye.engr.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/146/2021/03/Website_Considerations_for_Interconnection_of_Large_Systems.pdf)
- HVDC links "do not overload or propagate fault currents." Splitting a large AC grid into asynchronous segments via DC links "helps limit the propagation of disturbances and reduce the risk of cascading outages." — [EPFL, HVDC grid segmentation study](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/items/e71ff490-97c7-4848-b627-570b71974c8e)
- **Contrasting view:** former FERC Chair Jon Wellinghoff claimed that knocking out about nine critical substations across the three interconnections could black out the entire US, possibly for 18 months, citing transformer lead times. This is a physical-attack scenario, from a 2014 internal FERC analysis that was never publicly validated. — [Energy News Beat](https://energynewsbeat.co/electrical-generation/what-if-9-substations-were-targeted-on-the-us-grid-would-jon-wellinghoffs-prediction-of-an-18-month-us-grid-blackout-happen/); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/small-scale-power-grid-attack-could-cause-nationwide-blackout-study-says.amp)
- NERC's own risk framing is regional and assessment-area specific: 13 of 23 areas are elevated or high, and adequacy is measured against a 1-day-in-10-years loss-of-load criterion, meaning rotating outages, not system collapse. — [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/nerc-warns-long-term-grid-reliability-risks-mounting/)
- The largest data-center event so far (about 4 GW, July 2026) moved frequency to only 60.092 Hz, with no reliability impact. — [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/fault-in-data-center-alley-triggered-3-gw-load-drop-on-pjm)

### Inferences
- **The plausible worst cases are regional.** One is controlled load-shed or rotating outages in PJM, ERCOT or MISO during extreme heat or cold, if forecast data-center load materializes before supply. Another is an uncontrolled cascading blackout within one interconnection, of the scale of 2003 in the Northeast. That would require several failures to stack up, such as a large correlated data-center load loss coinciding with other contingencies and protection misoperation. NERC and FERC are now regulating to prevent that.
- **A simultaneous collapse of all three interconnections from load growth has no supporting technical analysis in the sources found.** Describing the risk as "nationwide grid collapse" would overstate the evidence. "Widespread regional shortfall risk across many regions" is accurate.
- **ERCOT is the most exposed interconnection to a single large-load event.** It is islanded, with low inertia and little import capability. The trade-off is that ERCOT also cannot spread its problems to the other two interconnections.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2025–2026) IEEE or NERC paper explicitly modeling a multi-interconnection cascade from data-center load.
- I did not retrieve the current total capacity of the East-West and ERCOT DC ties (historically on the order of about 1–2 GW).
