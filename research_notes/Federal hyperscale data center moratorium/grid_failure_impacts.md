# Human, Economic, and National-Security Consequences of Large-Scale / Long-Duration US Grid Failures

Evidence-grading key used throughout:
- **[EMPIRICAL]** = measured/observed after a real event (official counts, peer-reviewed excess-mortality studies, measured outages).
- **[MODELED-CREDIBLE]** = peer-reviewed or institutional modeling with stated methods (e.g., Stone et al. 2023, Lloyd's/Cambridge, LBNL).
- **[SPECULATIVE]** = worst-case assertions with thin or undisclosed methodology (e.g., EMP Commission 90% mortality).

## 1. Historical blackouts: human toll and economic cost

### Takeaway
Real US grid failures have killed dozens to hundreds (and, with Puerto Rico, thousands), and official death counts systematically undercount relative to excess-mortality studies by roughly 3x (Uri) to 15x (2003 NYC) to 46x (Maria). Economic costs run from billions (2003, Iberia) to $80-130B (Uri).

### Cited Findings
**2003 Northeast blackout (Aug 14-15, 2003; ~50-55 million people)**
- [EMPIRICAL] Peer-reviewed study (Anderson & Bell, *Epidemiology*) found NYC accidental deaths rose 122% and non-accidental (disease) deaths 25% during the blackout, ~90 excess deaths — vs. the NYC health department's initial attribution of only 6 deaths (mostly CO poisoning). — [Yale Bell Lab](https://bell-lab.yale.edu/publications/lights-out-impact-august-2003-power-outage-mortality-new-york-ny); [ASPR TRACIE](https://asprtracie.hhs.gov/technical-resources/resource/4022/lights-out-impact-of-the-august-2003-power-outage-on-mortality-in-new-york-ny); [Yale Environment Review](https://environment-review.yale.edu/node/3356)
- [MODELED-CREDIBLE] Anderson Economic Group estimated ~$6.4 billion reduction in US earnings; often cited as "~$6 billion." — [Anderson Economic Group](https://www.andersoneconomicgroup.com/northeast-blackout-likely-to-reduce-us-earnings-by-6-4-billion-aeg-working-paper-2003-2/)
- Root cause widely described as an overgrown tree contact in Ohio plus a software alarm failure, cascading to ~50 million people. — [Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/08/how-one-overgrown-tree-branch-in-ohio-blacked-out-50-million-people/); [bad.technology](https://bad.technology/articles/northeast-blackout-2003-software-alarm-failure-leaves-55-million-without-power.html)

**Winter Storm Uri, Texas (Feb 2021)**
- [EMPIRICAL – official] Texas DSHS final count: 246 deaths (released Dec 30, 2021). — [Texas Public Radio](https://www.tpr.org/texas/2022-01-03/texas-puts-final-estimate-of-winter-storm-death-toll-at-246); [KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/texas/2022/01/02/texas-puts-final-estimate-of-winter-storm-death-toll-at-246/)
- [EMPIRICAL – excess mortality, journalistic statistical analysis] BuzzFeed News estimated 702 excess deaths (range 426-978); statistician Ariel Karlinsky separately estimated 814. These are roughly 3x the official count. — [Texas Standard](https://texasstandard.org/stories/buzzfeed-report-estimates-winter-storm-deaths-at-least-triple-that-of-texas-count/)
- More than 4.5 million homes and businesses lost power, some for days; a University of Houston survey found 69% of Texans lost power at some point Feb 14-20 and ~49% had water-service disruptions. — [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/fiscal-notes/archive/2021/oct/winter-storm-impact.php)
- Over 14 million Texans were under boil-water notices across 190 counties (cascading water failure). — [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/texas-boil-water-notices-winter-storm/?ftag=CNM-00-10aab4i); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/14m-texas-residents-still-under-boil-water-notices-as-tens-of-thousands-lack-running-water)
- [MODELED-CREDIBLE] Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas estimated Texas losses of $80-130 billion. — [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/fiscal-notes/archive/2021/oct/winter-storm-impact.php)

**Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico (Sept 2017) — longest blackout in US history**
- [EMPIRICAL – excess mortality] George Washington University (Milken Institute SPH), commissioned by the Puerto Rico government: 2,975 excess deaths Sept 2017-Feb 2018, ~46x the official toll (64). Became the official estimate. — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/miami/news/new-study-estimates-nearly-3000-hurricane-maria-deaths-in-puerto-rico); [WTVR](https://www.wtvr.com/2018/08/28/hurricane-maria-caused-2975-deaths-in-puerto-rico-new-study)
- [EMPIRICAL – survey extrapolation, wide CI] Harvard household survey in *NEJM* (3,299 households): 4,645 excess deaths Sept 20-Dec 31, 2017 (midpoint; wide confidence interval). — [ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/national/hurricane-maria-death-toll)
- Interruption of medical care was the leading driver, particularly for chronically ill people dependent on electricity-requiring equipment and medicines. — [ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/national/hurricane-maria-death-toll)

**Winter Storm Elliott (Dec 2022)**
- [EMPIRICAL] FERC-NERC final report: 90,500 MW of coincident unplanned generation outages/derates/failures to start at the worst point; ~127,000 MW unavailable total (~18% of US generation); multiple operators shed firm load. TVA had the worst impact: nearly 8 hours of load shedding, peaking at 3,000 MW. Natural gas units failed the most. — [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/policy-regulation/ferc-nerc-final-report-natural-gas-struggled-the-most-during-winter-storm-elliott/); [FERC](https://www.ferc.gov/news-events/news/elliott-report-complete-electricity-standards-implement-gas-reliability-rules); [APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/node/22602)

**Hurricane Beryl, Houston (July 2024)**
- [EMPIRICAL] More than 2.2 million CenterPoint customers lost power (2.7 million+ Gulf Coast customers overall) during extreme heat and humidity. — [Wikipedia: Effects of Hurricane Beryl in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_Hurricane_Beryl_in_Texas); [KHOU](https://khou.com/article/weather/hurricane/beryl/beryl-power-outages-houston-heat-deaths/285-9d4183f6-7d40-4221-b462-d21e06a45bda)
- [EMPIRICAL] The outages contributed to at least ~10 deaths from heat or failed medical equipment; the Harris County Institute of Forensic Sciences confirmed at least 4 Houston residents and 2 Pasadena men died of hyperthermia during the outages. Class actions seeking $100M+ each were filed against CenterPoint. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_Hurricane_Beryl_in_Texas); [WHAS11/TEGNA](https://whas11.com/article/news/nation-world/hurricane-beryl-heat-deaths/285-ef2e71fc-1909-46b3-8741-ab9cb570d289)

**Iberian Peninsula blackout (Apr 28, 2025) — international comparator**
- [EMPIRICAL] ~60 million people in mainland Spain and Portugal lost power at 12:33 CEST; most areas restored in ~10 hours (6-24 hours range). ENTSO-E's investigation (75+ experts, 300+ GB data) described a rapid cascade. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Iberian_Peninsula_blackout); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/04/29/power-returns-after-massive-blackout-in-spain-and-portugal-as-fallout-continues)
- [EMPIRICAL, preliminary] At least 7 deaths in Spain and 1 in Portugal possibly linked (candle fires, generator fumes). [MODELED] Economic losses estimated at €1.6-4.5 billion; Volkswagen Navarra lost output of ~1,400 vehicles. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Iberian_Peninsula_blackout)

### Inferences
- Death tolls scale with **duration and season more than with geographic size**: a ~10-hour, mild-weather blackout covering 60 million people (Iberia) killed fewer than 10; a multi-day freeze (Uri) killed ~250-800; a months-long outage (Maria) killed ~3,000-4,600.
- Official counts consistently undercount; any argument should prefer peer-reviewed excess-mortality figures but present the official figures alongside.
- Many deaths are indirect (CO poisoning, hypothermia/hyperthermia, interrupted medical care, water loss), which is why cascading-dependency analysis matters.

### Gaps
- No reliable death toll found for Winter Storm Elliott in this research pass (US deaths are usually aggregated as storm deaths, not outage-specific). Total MW of firm load shed across all operators during Elliott (reported as ~5,400 MW in the FERC-NERC report) was not confirmed from a source fetched here.
- No peer-reviewed excess-mortality study for Beryl found; ~10 is likely a floor.
- Economic cost for Beryl/Elliott not located.

## 2. Modeled long-duration / wide-area blackout scenarios

### Takeaway
Credible modeling (Stone et al. 2023; Lloyd's/Cambridge; National Academies) supports thousands of deaths per affected city in a summer heat-wave blackout and $243B-$1T+ economic losses for a multi-state cyber blackout. The EMP Commission's "up to 90% of Americans dead within a year" is a speculative upper bound and should be presented as such.

### Cited Findings
**Heat wave + blackout (Stone et al. 2023, *Environmental Science & Technology*)** [MODELED-CREDIBLE, peer-reviewed]
- Modeling a 2006-type Phoenix heat wave (afternoon average 113°F for 5 days) plus a simultaneous multi-day blackout: more than half of Phoenix's population would need emergency medical care for heat illness, and about 1% of the population (~13,000; reported elsewhere as 12,800) would die. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/phoenix/2023/05/24/heat-wave-black-out-phoenx-heat-deaths-hospital); [Wyoming Public Media/NPR](https://www.wyomingpublicmedia.org/news/2023-06-15/blackouts-during-heat-waves-could-have-dire-public-health-consequences-study-shows); [Fox10 Phoenix](https://fox10phoenix.com/news/study-predicts-12800-deaths-if-heat-wave-and-power-failure-struck-phoenix)
- Death rates would more than double in Atlanta and Detroit, rising ~700% in Phoenix; Atlanta and Phoenix would not have enough hospital beds. Lead author Brian Stone (Georgia Tech). — [Cronkite News](https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2023/06/05/study-phoenix-faces-health-crisis-if-heatwave-blackout-hit-at-same-time); [Arizona Medical Association](https://www.azmed.org/news/641491/Combined-Heat-Wave-Blackout-Would-Send-Half-of-Phoenix-to-ER.htm)

**Lloyd's / Cambridge Centre for Risk Studies "Business Blackout" (2015)** [MODELED-CREDIBLE, stress-test scenario, not a forecast]
- Hypothetical cyberattack on generators blacks out 15 states plus DC, 93 million people. Base case US economic impact $243 billion; extreme variant over $1 trillion. Insurance claims $21.4B base, up to $71.1B. Expected effects include rising mortality as health/safety systems fail, port shutdowns, water-supply disruption as electric pumps fail, and transport chaos. — [Lloyd's press release](https://www.lloyds.com/news-and-risk-insight/press-releases/2015/07/business-blackout); [Claims Journal](https://claimsjournal.com/research/?p=10358); [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/grid-innovations/smart-grid/article/20965565/insurance-study-uses-us-power-grid-in-cyber-attack-scenario)

**National Academies, *Terrorism and the Electric Power Delivery System* (2012; written 2007, delayed by classification review)** [EXPERT ASSESSMENT]
- A terrorist attack could cause damage worse than natural disasters, blacking out large regions for weeks or months. High-voltage transformers are the single most vulnerable component: hard to move, not stockpiled in large numbers, and replacing many could take months or longer. — [National Academies news](https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2012/11/electric-power-grid-inherently-vulnerable-to-terrorist-attacks-report-delayed-in-classification-review-will-be-updated); [NAP OpenBook](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/12050/chapter/3)

**National Academies, *Enhancing the Resilience of the Nation's Electricity System* (2017)** [EXPERT ASSESSMENT]
- Focus: "large-area, long-duration outages", meaning blackouts across multiple service areas lasting several days or longer. The grid remains vulnerable to natural disasters, physical and cyber attacks, and accidents that could cause prolonged outages costing billions of dollars and lives, threatening health and safety and potentially national security. No single entity is responsible for planning, operating, or regulating the grid. — [National Academies](https://nationalacademies.org/news/2017/07/enhancing-the-resilience-of-the-nations-electricity-system); [NAP OpenBook ch.](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24836/chapter/10)

**EMP Commission "up to 90% mortality"** [SPECULATIVE]
- The claim: the Congressional EMP Commission (2008 era) estimated that within 12 months of a nationwide, long-term blackout, up to 90% of the US population could die from starvation, disease, and societal collapse. The reasoning is that EMP would push US technology back a century and population would shrink to what that technology could support. — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/brucedorminey/2017/10/23/north-korea-emp-attack-would-cause-mass-u-s-starvation-says-congressional-report/); [Peter Pry House testimony, Oct 2017](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/HM/HM09/20171012/106467/HHRG-115-HM09-Wstate-PryP-20171012.pdf); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2017/10/17/north-korea-emp-attack-warning)
- The figure is mostly spread through testimony and op-eds by commission leaders William Graham and Peter Vincent Pry ("death within a year of up to 90% of all Americans"), not through a published quantitative model. — [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/blog/buzz/millions-americans-could-die-are-we-ready-emp-attack-89926); [POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/expect-death-if-pulse-event-hits-power-grid/)
- Critiques: Kyle Mizokami (*Popular Mechanics*, "No, North Korea Can't Kill 90 Percent of Americans") called the claim "not realistic." Jeffrey Lewis (*Foreign Policy*, "The EMPire Strikes Back") called EMP "the conservative fetish that just won't die." Proponents in turn accuse critics of misquoting the commission. — [search summary of these articles; originals not fetched]; [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/blog/buzz/threat-emp-attack-very-real-105112?page=0%2C1)
- Much of the 90% figure's circulation online runs through low-credibility outlets (e.g., emp.news, survivalist Substacks), which should not be cited. — [emp.news example](https://emp.news/2015-11-12-90-percent-of-americans-would-die-in-two-years-without-electricity-warns-emp-commission.html)

### Inferences
- A defensible argument: "credible peer-reviewed modeling shows a multi-day summer blackout in one hot city could kill ~1% of residents and overwhelm hospitals." The EMP 90% figure is an unquantified worst-case judgment and should be framed as "the most extreme claim made by a congressional commission, disputed by analysts," not as an estimate.
- Transformer replacement lead times (months or longer) are the key mechanism that turns a days-long blackout into a weeks-to-months one in attack scenarios. This is what separates historical tolls from catastrophic modeled ones.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve the EMP Commission's own methodology text or a formal peer-reviewed critique; POWER Magazine page was blocked. No GAO or FEMA quantitative casualty model for a nationwide outage was found in this pass.
- The Stone et al. paper itself was not fetched; figures come from consistent press coverage quoting the authors.

## 3. Cascading infrastructure dependencies

### Takeaway
Electricity is the "lifeline" sector: real events show water systems (Uri: 14M on boil-water notices), medical care (Maria), and fuel/gas supply (Elliott, Uri) fail along with the grid. Nuclear plants and military bases depend on offsite grid power, with backup capacity designed for hours to days, not weeks.

### Cited Findings
- **Water**: During Uri, 14+ million Texans across 190 counties lacked safe water; ~49% of Texans had water disruptions. — [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/texas-boil-water-notices-winter-storm/?ftag=CNM-00-10aab4i); [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/fiscal-notes/archive/2021/oct/winter-storm-impact.php). Lloyd's scenario also models water disruption as electric pumps fail. — [Lloyd's](https://www.lloyds.com/news-and-risk-insight/press-releases/2015/07/business-blackout)
- **Health care**: Maria's excess deaths were driven chiefly by interrupted medical care and loss of power to medical equipment. — [ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/national/hurricane-maria-death-toll). Beryl deaths included failures of powered medical equipment. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_Hurricane_Beryl_in_Texas). Stone et al. projected hospital bed shortfalls in Phoenix and Atlanta. — [Cronkite News](https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2023/06/05/study-phoenix-faces-health-crisis-if-heatwave-blackout-hit-at-same-time)
- **Fuel / gas-electric interdependence**: In Elliott, natural gas generation had the most failures, partly from fuel-supply problems in cold weather; this two-way gas-electric coupling worsens outages. — [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/policy-regulation/ferc-nerc-final-report-natural-gas-struggled-the-most-during-winter-storm-elliott/)
- **Food / supply chain**: The EMP Commission's mortality mechanism rests on failure of food distribution (refrigeration, transport, fuel). — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/brucedorminey/2017/10/23/north-korea-emp-attack-would-cause-mass-u-s-starvation-says-congressional-report/) [SPECULATIVE at the 90% scale]
- **Ports/transport/trade**: Modeled port shutdowns and transport collapse in Lloyd's scenario. — [Lloyd's](https://www.lloyds.com/news-and-risk-insight/press-releases/2015/07/business-blackout)
- **Nuclear plants**: At Fukushima Daiichi, loss of offsite power plus loss of emergency diesels for ~9 days led to core melts in Units 1-3; the original 8-hour restoration assumption failed. A 2011 NRC task force recommended 8 hours of on-site power plus capability to cool cores and spent-fuel pools for 72 hours, noting 93 US reactors were then designed to cope with only a 4-hour station blackout. — [NRC ML11178A110](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1117/ML11178A110.pdf); [Christian Science Monitor](https://m.csmonitor.com/USA/2011/0713/Nuclear-safety-review-how-US-plants-could-handle-a-Fukushima-type-event). Spent-fuel pool loss of cooling is a recognized radiological-release risk. — [Friends of the Earth brief](https://foe.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/legacy/Issue_brief_Fukushima_current_status_and_lessons.pdf) (advocacy source; treat cautiously)
- **Military**: see Section 4.

### Inferences
- Duration thresholds matter: backup generation at hospitals, water plants, telecom sites, and nuclear plants is generally sized for hours to a few days (fuel-limited), so outages longer than about 72 hours produce compounding failures. This is consistent with NRC's 72-hour benchmark, but sector-specific backup durations were not sourced here.
- The 4-hour nuclear station-blackout figure is from 2011. Post-Fukushima NRC "FLEX" mitigation requirements have since been implemented, so current coping capability is longer; the 2011 figure should not be presented as current.

### Gaps
- No sourced quantitative data in this pass on telecom/cell tower backup duration, financial-system dependence, or cold-chain food loss during US outages.
- Current post-FLEX NRC coping times not retrieved.

## 4. National-security framings

### Takeaway
US agencies assess with high confidence that PRC-backed Volt Typhoon has pre-positioned in US energy, water, communications, and transport networks to disrupt them in a conflict, and DoD installations draw nearly all their power from the commercial grid. Together these make grid failure a direct military-readiness risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Volt Typhoon** (CISA/NSA/FBI advisory AA24-038A, Feb 2024): compromised IT environments of critical infrastructure organizations primarily in the Communications, Energy, Transportation, and Water/Wastewater sectors in the continental US and territories including Guam. Behavior is "not consistent with traditional cyber espionage"; agencies assess with high confidence that the actors are pre-positioning to move laterally to OT assets to disrupt functions "in the event of potential geopolitical tensions and/or military conflicts." Access maintained in some victims for at least five years. — [CISA AA24-038A](https://cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-038a); [TechTarget](https://techtarget.com/searchSecurity/news/366569227/CISA-Volt-Typhoon-had-access-to-some-US-targets-for-5-years)
- **DoD dependence** (GAO-10-147 and related): 99% of the energy consumed by DoD installations is generated off-installation, and 85% of the energy infrastructure DoD relies on is commercially owned. The Defense Science Board found "critical national security and homeland defense missions are at an unacceptably high risk of extended outage from failure of the grid." Listed threats include aging infrastructure, cyber-vulnerable automated controls, terrorist targeting, and long replacement lead times for key equipment. — [GAO-10-147](https://gao.gov/products/gao-10-147); [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/dod-reliance-commercial-power-grid-spells-trouble-gao-says); [FAS-hosted GAO PDF](https://sgp.fas.org/gao/electrical.pdf)
- Atlantic Council discusses rising electricity demand straining dual military/civilian needs. — [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/energysource/electricity-demand-is-rising-how-can-the-us-secure-energy-for-its-dual-military-and-civilian-needs/)
- National Academies (2017) states that large-area, long-duration outages could compromise national security. — [National Academies](https://nationalacademies.org/news/2017/07/enhancing-the-resilience-of-the-nations-electricity-system)

### Inferences
- Relevant to a data-center moratorium argument: adding large new loads that reduce reserve margins could plausibly raise the probability or severity of load shed during stress events (Uri/Elliott-type). This is an inference; it was not directly sourced here and belongs to adjacent research.
- The GAO 99% figure dates to 2009-2010. DoD has since invested in microgrids and installation energy resilience, so present it as a historical baseline.

### Gaps
- No updated (2020s) GAO figure on DoD grid dependence was retrieved. Volt Typhoon follow-on reporting (e.g., 2025 utility intrusions) was not covered in this pass.

## 5. Annual cost of power outages to the US economy (LBNL)

### Takeaway
LBNL's best estimate is ~$44 billion per year (2015$) for sustained interruptions in normal years. It excludes catastrophic long-duration events, so it is a floor for "ordinary" reliability costs, not a catastrophe estimate.

### Cited Findings
- [MODELED-CREDIBLE] LBNL (Larsen et al., *Energy*, June 2018): sustained interruptions (>5 min) cost US customers ~$44 billion/year (2015$), 25% above the 2006 estimate ($26B in 2002$ ≈ $35B in 2015$). Commercial customers bear 70%, industrial 27%, residential 3%. — [LBNL news](https://emp.lbl.gov/news/berkeley-lab-estimates-sustained-electric); [LBNL publication](https://eta.lbl.gov/publications/improving-estimated-cost-sustained)

### Inferences
- Single catastrophic events dwarf this annual baseline: Uri alone ($80-130B) was about 2-3x a full year of "normal" US outage costs, and the Lloyd's extreme scenario (>$1T) is about 20x.
- LBNL's customer-damage-function (ICE Calculator) method is known to be less reliable for multi-day outages, since its underlying surveys mostly cover interruptions of up to about a day.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve the LBNL ICE Calculator 2.0 updates or newer estimates (some recent non-LBNL estimates put annual outage costs at $100B+, but none were verified here).
