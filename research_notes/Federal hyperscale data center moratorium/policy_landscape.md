# Policy and Legal Landscape for a US Federal Moratorium on Hyperscale/AI Data Center Construction (as of 2 Oct 2026)

Method note: Several primary sites (sanders.senate.gov, congress.gov text pages, govinfo, troutman.com, dataforprogress.org, electricchoice.com) were blocked by the network egress proxy, so most findings below come from search-result summaries of those pages and secondary reporting. Exact bill text could not be read directly; treat wording-level details as reported, not verified against the statute text.

## 1. What federal moratorium proposals exist?

### Takeaway
The main federal vehicle is the **Artificial Intelligence Data Center Moratorium Act** (S.4214, Sanders, introduced 25 Mar 2026; H.R.9442, Ocasio-Cortez, introduced 24 Jun 2026; about 13 House cosponsors). It is a conditional, open-ended pause. It covers AI data centers with a peak load above 20 MW and lasts until Congress enacts broad AI, labor, ratepayer and environmental safeguards. It also includes chip export restrictions. It has not advanced. It is backed by a Dec 2025 letter from more than 230 groups, and Rep. Frank Pallone (top Democrat on Energy & Commerce) endorsed a national moratorium in June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **S.4214, introduced 25 Mar 2026** by Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT), announced jointly with Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY). It would halt construction of AI infrastructure until lawmakers enact measures requiring government review of AI products, preventing mass job displacement, and limiting consumer electricity price increases. — [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5801008-ai-data-center-moratorium-act/); [Axios, 25 Mar 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/25/sanders-aoc-data-center-moratorium-bill); [Sanders press release](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/press-releases/news-sanders-ocasio-cortez-announce-ai-data-center-moratorium-act/); [Congress.gov S.4214 text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4214/text)
- **Definition and scope (as reported).** An "AI data center" is a facility, or connected facilities, used to develop or operate AI models at scale, or with a total peak power load above **20 megawatts** that uses high-performance server racks. The bill would immediately halt new **and expanded** AI data center construction. — [Section-by-section (Sanders)](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Artificial-Intelligence-Data-Center-Moratorium-Act-Section-by-Section.pdf); [Bill text PDF](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/AI-Data-Center-Moratorium.-FINAL-Text.pdf); [Roll Call, 25 Mar 2026](https://rollcall.com/2026/03/25/data-center-moratorium-pitched-as-counter-to-ai-impacts/)
- **Conditions for lifting.** The moratorium ends only after federal legislation establishes all of the following:
  - review and approval of AI products for safety and effectiveness;
  - policies to prevent job displacement and share wealth;
  - rules for future data centers requiring no increase in consumer utility bills, no climate or environmental harm, local community approval power, no government subsidies, and union labor standards (prevailing wages, apprenticeships, project labor agreements).
  — [Section-by-section](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Artificial-Intelligence-Data-Center-Moratorium-Act-Section-by-Section.pdf); [Jacobin, Apr 2026](https://jacobin.com/2026/04/bernie-aoc-artificial-intelligence-regulation)
- **Export provision.** The bill directs the Commerce Department to prohibit exports of "computing infrastructure hardware" such as chips to countries without similar regulations. — [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/03/25/data-center-moratorium-pitched-as-counter-to-ai-impacts/); [S.4214 summary (legisletter)](https://legisletter.org/bill/s4214-artificial-intelligence-data-center-moratorium-act)
- **H.R.9442 (House companion).** Introduced by Rep. Ocasio-Cortez on 24 Jun 2026, with 9 original cosponsors and 13 by the time of the GovTrack snapshot:
  - Original cosponsors: Carson (IN-07), Cohen (TN-09), García (IL-04), Goldman (NY-10), Grijalva (AZ-07), McGovern (MA-02), Sewell (AL-07), Tlaib (MI-12), Watson Coleman (NJ-12).
  - Some sources differ on whether Sewell was an original cosponsor. AL Reporter (22 Jul 2026) reports her cosponsoring in July.
  — [GovTrack H.R.9442](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/hr9442); [Congress.gov cosponsors](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9442/cosponsors); [AL Reporter, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.alreporter.com/2026/07/22/rep-sewell-cosponsors-artificial-intelligence-data-center-moratorium-act/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ocasio-cortez-and-sanders-introduce-ai-data-center-moratorium-act/)
- **Status.** The bill has not become law, and no committee action was found. — [Newsweek / search summary](https://newsweek.com/ai-politics/the-data-center-election-12501378)
- **Pallone endorsement (June 2026).** Rep. Frank Pallone (D-NJ), ranking member of House Energy & Commerce, said at a committee markup: "I am in favor of a national AI data center moratorium until we can find a way to ensure they don't harm our nation's air, water and power bills." The Data Center Coalition (Cy McNeill) responded that a moratorium "risks rationing access to cloud and digital services, impairs our global competitiveness." — [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/pallone-calls-for-national-data-center-moratorium/); [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-democrats-call-for-data-center-moratorium-stokes-opposition); [Jersey Vindicator, 24 Jun 2026](https://jerseyvindicator.org/2026/06/24/u-s-rep-frank-pallone-backs-national-moratorium-on-ai-data-centers/)
- **Advocacy demand (8 Dec 2025).** More than 230 national, state and local organizations sent Congress a letter calling for a "full nationwide moratorium on the approval and construction of new data centers."
  - The letter was facilitated by Food & Water Watch.
  - Signers include Physicians for Social Responsibility, Greenpeace, Friends of the Earth, Progressive Democrats of America, Our Revolution and Americans for Financial Reform.
  - A parallel letter from 50+ New York groups was sent the same day.
  — [Food & Water Watch](https://www.foodandwaterwatch.org/2025/12/08/230-groups-call-for-national-moratorium-on-new-data-centers/); [NPR, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/12/09/nx-s1-5637532/environmental-groups-call-for-a-moratorium-on-data-center-construction); [FWW NY letter](https://www.foodandwaterwatch.org/2025/12/08/50-new-york-groups-call-for-moratorium-on-new-data-centers/)
- **Related non-moratorium federal bills.**
  - Sen. Jon Husted's (R-OH) **Ratepayer Protection Act** would have data centers or tech companies bear new grid infrastructure costs, but does not require states to adopt such standards. Sen. Martin Heinrich blocked it on unanimous consent in Sept 2026 as too weak.
  - Heinrich's own **GRID Savings Act** (large loads pay for their interconnection facilities) was blocked by Sen. Bernie Moreno.
  — [Roll Call, 17 Sep 2026](https://rollcall.com/2026/09/17/ratepayer-bill-aimed-at-data-center-buildout-blocked-in-senate/); [Washington Examiner](https://washingtonexaminer.com/news/senate/4748289/democrats-block-jon-husted-data-center-bill-ratepayer-protection-act)
- **Industry counter-mobilization (28 Sep 2026).** The **American Infrastructure Alliance** formed as a multimillion-dollar coalition to head off moratoria through 2027 "standards and guardrails."
  - Industry backers: Blackstone, OpenAI, QTS, SoftBank.
  - Union backers: IBEW, United Association, SMART, Iron Workers, HFIAW.
  - Target states: TX, GA, OH, IA, PA, IN, SC.
  - IBEW President Kenneth Cooper: "Blanket bans on necessary infrastructure projects would set back our economy and threaten good middle-class jobs."
  — [Axios, 28 Sep 2026](https://axios.com/2026/09/28/ai-industry-unions-data-centers)

### Inferences
- The Sanders/AOC bill works more like an indefinite pause tied to a sweeping AI regulatory package than a time-limited moratorium. Its lifting conditions (AI product pre-approval, job-displacement policy, no subsidies, PLAs) make it a platform document unlikely to pass a Republican Congress.
- Building trades unions sided with industry, which splits the traditional Democratic coalition and is a political obstacle to the bill's labor-standards framing.
- The bill's 20 MW threshold lines up with the FERC/DOE "large load" definition and Maine's LD 307. 20 MW is emerging as the de facto policy threshold.

### Gaps
- Exact statutory text (sections, enforcement mechanism, penalties, which agency enforces, treatment of projects under construction) could not be verified because congress.gov and govinfo were blocked.

- No evidence was found of a formal PauseAI or AI-safety-group legislative proposal for a data center moratorium. One source says "AI-safety campaigners" support H.R.9442, but specific groups were not identified.
- Presidential-campaign platforms (2028 hopefuls) were not researched.

## 2. What state, local and international moratoria exist?

### Takeaway
No statewide moratorium is in force. Maine's LD 307 (20 MW+, until 1 Nov 2027) passed in April 2026 but was vetoed, and the override failed on 29 Apr 2026. Local moratoria have proliferated, with more than 100 localities by mid-2026 and roughly 12 states considering statewide bills. International precedents (Dublin 2021–Dec 2025, Amsterdam 2019–2020, Singapore 2019–Jan 2022) all ended in conditional reopening rather than permanent bans.

### Cited Findings
- **Maine LD 307 (proposed, failed).** The bill would have barred municipalities and state agencies from permitting data centers with a load of 20 MW or more until 1 Nov 2027, and created a Maine Data Center Coordination Council.
  - The Legislature passed it around 9 Apr 2026.
  - Gov. Janet Mills vetoed it on 24 Apr 2026 because it lacked an exemption for a pending project in Jay, Maine. She said a moratorium "is appropriate."
  - The override failed on 29 Apr 2026: the House voted 72–65 (short of two-thirds) and the Senate voted 20–11 to sustain the veto.
  — [Maine Morning Star, 9 Apr](https://mainemorningstar.com/2026/04/09/landmark-data-center-moratorium-passes-maine-legislature/); [Governor's veto message](https://www.maine.gov/governor/mills/official_documents/veto_messages/2026-04-ld-307-act-establish-maine-data-center-coordination); [Maine Morning Star, 29 Apr](https://mainemorningstar.com/2026/04/29/despite-initial-support-legislature-fails-to-override-mills-veto-of-landmark-data-center-ban/); [WABI](https://www.wabi.tv/2026/04/29/maine-legislature-does-not-override-gov-mills-veto-data-center-moratorium/)
- **Local moratoria (enacted, 2026).**

  - Georgia: 52 counties and cities have moratoria or ordinances.
  - Indiana: more than one-third of 92 counties restrict data centers, and Indianapolis enacted a moratorium through 2027 in Aug 2026.

- "More than 100 local communities ... have enacted moratoriums on data centers, and 12 states are pushing forward with statewide moratorium proposals." This is reported in coverage of H.R.9442 and likely echoes sponsor claims. — [DebateUS / search summary](https://debateus.org/data-centers-scouting-report-and-file-august-31-2026/)
- **Ohio public opinion.** A Bowling Green State University poll found 78% of Ohio residents favor a moratorium on new data center construction while more information is gathered. — [Newsweek](https://newsweek.com/ai-politics/the-data-center-election-12501378)
- **Ireland (international precedent).**
  - EirGrid/CRU imposed a de facto moratorium on new Dublin-area grid connections from 2021.
  - CRU lifted it in Dec 2025 with a new Large Energy User connection policy. New data centers must:
    - have on-site generation or storage able to meet their full demand;
    - match at least 80% of annual demand with additional Irish renewables (six-year glide path);
    - supply power back to the grid when needed.
  - Data centers use 21% of Ireland's metered electricity.
  — [Bloomberg, 12 Dec 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-12/ireland-set-to-end-moratorium-on-new-power-links-to-data-centers); [RTÉ, 12 Dec 2025](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2025/1212/1548674-80-of-data-centre-energy-must-come-from-renewables-cru/)
- **Amsterdam and Haarlemmermeer.** The two municipalities announced an immediate ban on new data center developments in July 2019 and lifted it about a year later under new zoning and sustainability rules. Market effects persisted for years. — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/amsterdam-pauses-data-center-building/); [DCD analysis](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/analysis/the-ongoing-impact-of-amsterdams-data-center-moratorium); [Dutch Data Center Association](https://www.dutchdatacenters.nl/en/nieuws/amsterdam_moratorium_jul20/)
- **Singapore.** A moratorium imposed in 2019 was lifted in Jan 2022 with conditions: about 60 MW of new capacity per year, allocated in 10–30 MW tranches, and PUE of 1.3 or lower. — [W.Media](https://w.media/singapore-data-centre-moratorium-lifted-what-next/); [DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/analysis/after-the-moratorium-how-singapore-plans-to-stay-in-data-center-race)

### Inferences
- The international record suggests moratoria work as a "pause-then-conditional-reopen" tool. Each ended within 1–4 years and was replaced by allocation, efficiency or self-supply conditions, not permanent bans.
- In the US, local zoning power is where moratoria actually get enacted. Statewide action has stalled on project-specific carve-out fights (Maine/Jay).

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence of effect (for example, MW displaced or projects relocated) was found for US local moratoria.
- No Virginia (Loudoun/Prince William), Arizona (Tucson/Chandler) or Texas-specific moratorium details were retrieved.
- The list of the "12 states" with statewide bills was not verified (NY, PA and others are likely but unconfirmed).

## 3. Federal legal authority and obstacles

### Takeaway
Congress could plausibly legislate a moratorium under the Commerce Clause, since data centers are interstate-commerce facilities connected to the interstate grid. No existing executive authority clearly allows a construction ban. FERC's jurisdiction over large-load interconnection was only being asserted in 2026, and narrowly. DOE 202(c) is limited to temporary emergencies. NEPA and EPA levers apply only where there is a federal nexus. The federal executive is pushing the opposite direction.

### Cited Findings
- **FERC large-load docket.**
  - Oct 2025: DOE Secretary Wright used FPA §403 to direct FERC to start a rulemaking (ANOPR) on interconnecting large loads (preliminarily over 20 MW) to the transmission system. Comments were due 14 Nov 2025 and reply comments 28 Nov 2025, and DOE asked for final action by 30 Apr 2026.
  - 16 Apr 2026: FERC issued an order committing to act by end of June 2026.
  - 18 Jun 2026: Instead of a final rule, FERC issued six FPA §206 show-cause orders (PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO) requiring each RTO to show its large-load interconnection tariff is just and reasonable.
  - The federal–state jurisdictional boundary (retail and siting are state matters) is the central legal issue. Commenters proposed thresholds from 30 MW to 300 MW.
  — [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/speaking-energy/doe-directs-ferc-to-initiate-large-load-interconnection-rulemaking); [Holland & Knight, Apr 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/ferc-to-act-on-large-load-interconnection-docket-in-june); [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/ferc-sets-june-action-on-does-large-load-interconnection-plan-putting-federal-state-boundaries-at-the-center-of-what-comes-next/); [hData](https://blog.hdata.com/2026-ferc-rulemaking-large-loads); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/ferc-large-load-interconnection-preliminary-rulemaking-key-takeaways-for-data-center-developers-other-large-load-projects-and-investors)
- **DOE FPA §202(c).** The statute lets the Secretary order temporary connections, generation and delivery during emergencies caused by a sudden increase in demand or a shortage of energy or facilities. Orders conflicting with environmental law must be limited to the hours necessary.
  - DOE has used it **for** data center operations, not against them. Order No. 202-26-23 (18 May 2026) let PJM direct data center backup generation to run before or during EEA-3 conditions.
  - A later order reportedly let PJM curtail data centers of 50 MW or more onto backup power during a July 2026 heat wave.
  — [CRS R48568](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48568); [CRS IF12991](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12991/IF12991.2.pdf); [DOE Order 202-26-23](https://www.energy.gov/documents/doe-emergency-order-no-202-26-23pdf); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/doe-emergency-order-signals-new-role-for-data-centers-backup-power/); [mgrid, 30 Jun 2026](https://mgrid.org/2026/06/30/doe-emergency-order-lets-pjm-curtail-50-mw-plus-data-centers-to-backup-power-during-july-heat-wave/)
- **EPA on-site gas turbines.**
  - In Jan 2026 EPA confirmed that large methane gas turbines need permits, which was relevant to xAI's Memphis-area turbines.
  - EPA also finalized a turbine NSPS (15 Jan 2026) with a "temporary" subcategory (up to 850 MMBtu/hr, used less than 24 months) and a conditional "nonroad" exclusion that eases permitting.
  - In Sept 2026 the administration moved to ease environmental rules for data centers further.
  — [SELC, 15 Jan 2026](https://selc.org/press-release/epa-confirms-that-large-methane-gas-turbines-require-permits/); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/epa-s-new-turbine-rules-provide-air-9433112/); [Axios, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/10/trump-ease-environmental-rules-data-centers)
- **Commerce Clause.** One analysis notes a federal moratorium "rests on contestable Commerce Clause and federal-permitting hooks, which is why most real-world action has been at the state and local level." — [Debate Arguments substack](https://debatearguments.substack.com/p/data-center-moratorium-or-ban-what) (low-authority source)
- **Export controls.** S.4214 uses Commerce export authority (chips to non-equivalent countries) as an add-on. — [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/03/25/data-center-moratorium-pitched-as-counter-to-ai-impacts/)

### Inferences
- **Statute is the main legal route.** A moratorium by statute would likely survive Commerce Clause review, since large data centers are economic activity with substantial interstate effects. The main constitutional risks are:
  - anti-commandeering, if the statute directs state or local permitting officials (Maine's model directed municipalities, which a state can do but Congress cannot);
  - Takings claims for projects already in progress.
  Federal enforcement would more likely run through interconnection bans, federal permits, and conditions on funding.
- **Executive routes are weak.**
  - FERC's §206 orders concern interconnection terms and cannot halt retail-load siting; behind-the-meter or self-generated campuses would evade any interconnection lever.
  - §202(c) is temporary and emergency-only.
  - NEPA applies only to federal actions (federal land, federal funding, federal permits).
  - EPA Clean Air Act permitting of on-site gas could slow, but not ban, construction.
  These are my legal inferences. No law-firm analysis of a federal moratorium's constitutionality was directly retrieved.

### Gaps
- A Troutman Pepper Locke alert on the "temporary pause" (blocked) likely covers legal mechanisms; its content could not be read.
- No CRS report specifically on a federal data center moratorium was found.

## 4. Current federal policy direction and conflict with a moratorium

### Takeaway
The Trump administration is pushing hard in the opposite direction: expedited permitting (EO 14318, July 2025), using DOE to force faster grid interconnection, and loosening EPA rules. The Dec 2025 AI preemption EO, however, expressly does **not** preempt state or local data-center and compute-infrastructure laws, leaving local moratoria intact.

### Cited Findings
- **EO 14318, "Accelerating Federal Permitting of Data Center Infrastructure" (23 Jul 2025).** It streamlines environmental review and permitting and directs Commerce to provide financial support for qualifying projects. It does not preempt state permitting, zoning or energy rules. — [Columbia Sabin Center](https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/president-trump-orders-expedited-permitting-data-centers); [MultiState, 14 Apr 2026](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/14/federal-ai-data-center-policy-meets-resistance-from-state-lawmakers)
- **EO "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence" (11 Dec 2025).**
  - It directs DOJ litigation against state AI laws.
  - It expressly excludes from preemption state laws on child safety, "AI compute and data center infrastructure (except for generally applicable permitting reforms)," and state procurement.
  - States enacted more than a dozen data center laws in 2026.
  — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/president-trump-signs-executive-order-challenging-state-ai-laws); [Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ai-executive-order-targets-state-laws-and-seeks-uniform-federal-standards); [Columbia CGEP](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/trumps-ai-executive-order-wont-slow-the-surge-of-state-data-center-laws/)
- **Other administration actions.**
  - The DOE directive to FERC (Oct 2025) was explicitly aimed at **expediting** data center interconnection.
  - EPA eased turbine permitting (Jan 2026) and moved to loosen data center environmental rules (Sept 2026).
  — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/secretary-of-energy-directs-ferc-to-initiate-rulemaking-to-expedite-data-center-and-large-load-interconnection/); [Axios, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/10/trump-ease-environmental-rules-data-centers)

### Inferences
- A federal moratorium has no realistic path under the current administration and Congress. Its near-term significance is as a 2026 midterm and 2028 platform issue.
- The administration's carve-out for data center infrastructure laws means local moratoria are, for now, insulated from executive-order preemption, though not from future statutory preemption.

### Gaps
- The July 2025 AI Action Plan's specific data-center-on-federal-land provisions (DOE/DOI site selections) were not retrieved in this pass.

## 6. How "hyperscale" is defined and what a moratorium would cover

### Takeaway
"Hyperscale" has no statutory definition. The policy proposals use a **20 MW peak load** threshold: S.4214/H.R.9442 (paired with AI or high-performance hardware), Maine LD 307, and the DOE/FERC large-load ANOPR. Industry definitions use IDC's 5,000+ servers and 10,000+ sq ft. Typical hyperscale sites are 20–50 MW or more, and AI campuses now often run to hundreds of MW or GW scale.

### Cited Findings
- IDC: 5,000 or more servers and at least 10,000 sq ft. Typical hyperscale facilities draw 20–50 MW, with 10–25 kW per rack. — [Vertiv](https://www.vertiv.com/en-ca/about/news-and-events/articles/educational-articles/what-is-a-hyperscale-data-center/); [IBM](https://www.ibm.com/topics/hyperscale)
- S.4214: an AI data center is either used for AI models at scale, or has a peak load above 20 MW with high-performance racks. The bill also covers expansions. — [Section-by-section](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Artificial-Intelligence-Data-Center-Moratorium-Act-Section-by-Section.pdf)
- Maine LD 307: 20 MW or more. — [Maine Monitor](https://themainemonitor.org/maine-data-center-moratorium/)
- FERC/DOE: large load is preliminarily over 20 MW. Commenters noted this catches ordinary industrial customers and proposed 30–300 MW instead. — [hData](https://blog.hdata.com/2026-ferc-rulemaking-large-loads)

### Inferences
- A 20 MW threshold covers far more than "hyperscale," including many colocation and enterprise sites. A moratorium aimed at true hyperscale or AI campuses would need a higher MW cutoff (100 MW or more) or the AI-use and hardware test. The "AI use" prong in S.4214 adds definitional ambiguity that would invite litigation.

### Gaps
- No authoritative count of US projects above 20 MW or above 100 MW in the pipeline was retrieved, so the number of projects a moratorium would cover could not be quantified.
