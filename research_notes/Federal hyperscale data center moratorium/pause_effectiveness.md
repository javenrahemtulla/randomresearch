## Pro-pause arguments: what the main pause/prohibition proposals claim and why slowing would reduce risk

### Takeaway
The pro-pause camp has moved from a 6-month voluntary pause (FLI, March 2023) to calls for an internationally enforced prohibition on superintelligence with chip/compute controls (Yudkowsky 2023 and 2025 book; MIRI TGT draft treaty 2025; FLI Superintelligence Statement Oct 2025). Nearly all serious pro-pause proposals explicitly depend on international (especially US–China) participation and compute verification; none argues a unilateral US data center freeze alone suffices. By Sept 2026 even frontier lab CEOs (Amodei, Altman, Musk, Hassabis) endorsed "pacing" the frontier, but conditioned on multilateral coordination and explicitly not a halt.

### Cited Findings
- FLI open letter (March 22, 2023) called for "all AI labs to immediately pause for at least 6 months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4," with governments stepping in with a moratorium if labs would not. [background, not re-verified this session] — [FLI, Pause Giant AI Experiments](https://futureoflife.org/open-letter/pause-giant-ai-experiments/)
- Yudkowsky's TIME op-ed (March 29, 2023), "Pausing AI Developments Isn't Enough. We Need to Shut it All Down," called for an indefinite worldwide moratorium on large training runs, tracking of GPUs, caps on compute, and willingness to destroy rogue data centers by airstrike; no exceptions for governments or militaries. [background, not re-verified this session] — [TIME](https://time.com/6266923/ai-eliezer-yudkowsky-open-letter-not-enough/)
- Yudkowsky & Soares, *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies* (Sept 2025): argues any ASI built with current techniques kills everyone; plan calls for outlawing unmonitored high-end GPU possession, and if any state builds a large data center in defiance of a universal ban, others should destroy it via sabotage or airstrikes. Yudkowsky: "If we get an effective international treaty shutting A.I. down, and the book had something to do with it, I'll call the book a success." — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/if-anyone-builds-it-everyone-dies-researchers-warn-as-they-call-for-global-ai-shutdown/)

- MIRI (March 18, 2026) summarized "Mechanisms to Verify International Agreements about AI Development" — tracking chip location, verifying tracked compute isn't used for large training runs, certifying evaluations. — [MIRI](https://intelligence.org/2026/03/18/mechanisms-to-verify-international-agreements-about-ai-development/)
- FLI "Statement on Superintelligence" (released Oct 22, 2025) calls for "a prohibition on the development of superintelligence, not lifted before there is broad scientific consensus that it will be done safely and controllably, and strong public buy-in." - PauseAI (founded 2023) organizes international protests calling for a global pause on frontier training; EA Forum discussion frames it as "activism under uncertainty." — [EA Forum: The International PauseAI Protest](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/eqTGrEsBzJJSiuTcv/the-international-pauseai-protest-activism-under-uncertainty)

### Inferences
- The strongest pro-pause case is not "a US moratorium on data centers reduces AGI risk" but "a verified, international compute cap reduces AGI risk." A domestic data center moratorium is at most a component (it demonstrates seriousness and constrains the single largest compute builder) and at worst decoupled from the real lever.
- The Sanders/AOC AI Data Center Moratorium Act's stated triggers (worker, civil-liberty, environmental safeguards) differ from the AGI-safety rationale; its AGI justification must borrow from the above literature, which itself insists on internationality.
- Industry convergence on "pacing" (Sept 2026) shifts the Overton window but its content (evaluations, time-to-align) is regulatory-gating, not a compute freeze.

## Against/complicating: race dynamics, overhang, displacement, and the "backfire" case

### Takeaway
The main objections are (1) a unilateral US slowdown cedes the frontier to China, whose domestic chip and power buildout is accelerating despite export controls; (2) pausing compute while hardware/algorithms keep improving creates an "overhang" that produces a faster, more dangerous jump later; (3) compute displaces to other jurisdictions (Gulf states) rather than disappearing; (4) pauses may lock in bad regulation and slow empirical alignment research (Belrose/Pope). Rebuttals (Grace, AI Impacts, MIRI, Bensinger) contest overhang's magnitude and argue the alternative—racing—is worse.

- OpenAI's "Planning for AGI and beyond" (Feb 2023) argued a gradual transition and shorter timelines with slower takeoff is preferable partly to avoid compute overhang; Anthropic's "Core Views on AI Safety" (March 2023) similarly argued safety research needs frontier models. ### Inferences
- A US-only data center moratorium is vulnerable on all four objections simultaneously: US labs could train abroad (UAE/Saudi capacity is explicitly US-aligned and growing), China's domestic chip/power buildout continues, chip production and algorithmic efficiency continue (overhang), and the policy's sunset condition (a "federal framework") is not tied to alignment milestones.
- Counterpoint in the moratorium's favor: a construction moratorium does not stop existing compute or chip production, so it delays scale-up more than it pauses research—arguably a "slowdown" with smaller overhang than a full training ban, but also weaker effect on risk.
- Note the overhang argument cuts less if the binding constraint is power/data center buildout (which can't be "stockpiled" the way algorithmic progress can) — but chips and algorithms can be stockpiled, so overhang still applies partly. This is an analytical inference, not a sourced claim.

## Would a pause need to be international? Verification, treaties, historical analogies

### Takeaway
Virtually all participants—pause advocates (MIRI, Yudkowsky, FLI), lab leaders (Hassabis, Amodei), and skeptics—agree an effective pause must be international with China. Verification via chip tracking/hardware mechanisms is judged "plausible but not yet possible" (RAND), TRL ~2–5. Historical moratoria (Asilomar rDNA, 2014–2017 gain-of-function pause) were short, voluntary, Western, and did not bind adversaries (e.g., USSR bioweapons continued).

### Cited Findings
- RAND/University of Bristol work "Verifying International Agreements on AI" breaks verification into R&D problems in hardware security, network analysis, sensors; proposes redundant layers from secure hardware to whistleblower programs; concludes a robust multi-layered verification regime is "plausible, but not yet possible," requiring directed research. — summary via [longtermwiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/E2053) (secondary aggregator); see also [alphaXiv 2507.15916](https://www.alphaxiv.org/audio/2507.15916)
- Hardware-enabled mechanisms taxonomy: location attestation, TEEs, compute metering, interconnect limits, chip tracking, remote disablement; field at TRL 2–5 and underfunded; key orgs CNAS, RAND, IAPS, FlexHEG. — [longtermwiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/resources/ab7f0c2b472816cf) (secondary aggregator)
- MIRI (Mar 2026) verification goals: track chip location; verify tracked compute isn't doing large-scale training; certify evaluations. — [MIRI](https://intelligence.org/2026/03/18/mechanisms-to-verify-international-agreements-about-ai-development/)
- Gain-of-function pause: White House announced in Oct 2014 a pause of new funding for GoF experiments on influenza, MERS, SARS, and asked ongoing ones to stop voluntarily (lifted Dec 2017 with the P3CO framework [lift date: background]). — [NIH OSP public comments document](https://osp.od.nih.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Gain_of_Function_Deliberative_Process_Written_Public_Comments.pdf)
- Asilomar: 1974 voluntary moratorium on certain rDNA experiments; 1975 conference concluded research should proceed under strict guidelines later formalized into NIH rules; the pause lasted only months, and did not stop USSR DNA research in its secret bioweapons program. — [longtermwiki: pause/moratorium](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/responses/alignment/policy/pause-moratorium); Grace also wrote an Asilomar case study for MIRI (2015). — [Katja Grace podcast](https://hearthisidea.com/episodes/grace/)
- Hassabis (pre-2026): "you need the whole world to pause, including China"; a pause could make things worse if everything else carries on. — [Liron Shapira Substack](https://lironshapira.substack.com/p/special-report-google-deepminds-frontier) (snippet)
- Yudkowsky/Soares and MIRI explicitly frame success as an international treaty with enforcement. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/if-anyone-builds-it-everyone-dies-researchers-warn-as-they-call-for-global-ai-shutdown/)

### Inferences
- Historical analogies favor pauses that are (a) narrow, (b) short, (c) aimed at a small, identifiable research community, and (d) paired with a clear exit criterion. AGI compute restrictions match (c) partially (few frontier labs, few chip fabs—TSMC/ASML chokepoints) but not (b) or (d). The nuclear test ban analogy (verifiable via seismic monitoring) is the closest to a verifiable compute cap; no source retrieved this session analyzed it in depth.
- A unilateral US moratorium could serve as a bargaining chip/"credible commitment" toward a treaty, but no source found argues this persuasively with evidence.

## What Chinese government/AI actors say about safety and pauses; US–China AI dialogue

As of late Sept 2026, Beijing publicly rejects US calls for an AI slowdown as a "Cold War playbook"/"malicious competition" aimed at hobbling China, while agreeing at the Trump–Xi summit (late Sept 2026) to a US–China "Super Intelligence Dialogue" and an AI incident-reporting channel—with no binding limits. Neither side committed to slowing the frontier.

## National security community views (RAND, CNAS, Superintelligence Strategy/MAIM)

### Takeaway
The dominant natsec view rejects a unilateral pause and favors competitiveness plus nonproliferation and deterrence. Hendrycks/Schmidt/Wang's "Superintelligence Strategy" (March 5, 2025) proposes MAIM—states deterring rivals' destabilizing AI projects via threat of sabotage—alongside chip nonproliferation and competitiveness, and opposes a US "Manhattan Project" for AGI. Critics say MAIM's red lines are unclear/unmonitorable and deterrence unstable. RAND/CNAS focus on verification and hardware mechanisms that could underpin future agreements.

### Cited Findings
- "Superintelligence Strategy" published March 5, 2025 by Dan Hendrycks, Eric Schmidt, Alexandr Wang; central concept Mutual Assured AI Malfunction (MAIM), analogous to MAD, deterring "destabilizing" AI via reciprocal sabotage; three pillars deterrence, nonproliferation, competitiveness. — [Zvi Mowshowitz, "On MAIM and Superintelligence Strategy"](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/on-maim-and-superintelligence-strategy); [TechCrunch, Mar 5, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/05/eric-schmidt-argues-against-a-manhattan-project-for-agi/); [The Register, Mar 6, 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/03/06/schmidt_ai_superintelligence/)
- Schmidt et al. argued a US AGI "Manhattan Project" would provoke preemptive sabotage by China ("get MAIM'd"). — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/03/06/schmidt_ai_superintelligence/)
- Critiques: MAIM lacks a Nash-equilibrium strategy and raises miscalculation risk; unclear/unmonitorable red lines, questionable credibility of sabotage, volatile deterrence calculus; assumes rivals would accept lasting imbalance. — [LessWrong: "The Jackpot Jinx"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JF2JABmeM9opszwZA/the-jackpot-jinx-or-why-superintelligence-strategy-is-wrong); [LessWrong: Refining MAIM](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/thDvcKMvBprn4rEwR/refining-maim-identifying-changes-required-to-meet); [The Counterfactual: MAIM is probably not a good deal for America](https://thecounterfactual.substack.com/p/maim-is-probably-not-a-good-deal)
- RAND verification research: multi-layer verification "plausible, but not yet possible." CNAS, RAND, IAPS named as leaders on hardware-enabled mechanisms. — [longtermwiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/E2053)
- US policy (Trump administration) explicitly prioritizes beating China; Bessent: "We can't pause." — [TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/09/18/us-china-ai-race-trump-bessent-lifeng-talks/)
- Notably, some natsec figures (Susan Rice, Adm. Mike Mullen) signed the Oct 2025 Superintelligence prohibition statement. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/prince-harry-meghan-join-global-call-to-ban-superintelligent-ai-over-human-extinction-fearsprince-harry-meghan-join-global-call-to-ban-superintelligent-ai-over-human-extinction-fear-82919.htm)

### Inferences
- Interestingly, MAIM logic implies large, visible data centers are targets/deterrence objects; restraint is framed as mutual and enforced by threat, not as unilateral self-restraint—consistent with the consensus that unilateral US moratoria are not a security-community-endorsed AGI risk tool.
- The natsec community's most "pause-compatible" contribution is verification infrastructure (chip tracking, location attestation), which a federal policy could advance without a construction moratorium.
