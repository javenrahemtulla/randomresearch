> Method note for the report writer: Research was done on 2026-10-02. Several primary sources (Utility Dive, RTO Insider, Canary Media, Grid Strategies PDFs, EPRI's Powering Intelligence site, Epoch AI) were blocked by the network proxy. ## 1. Current and projected data center share of US electricity

### Takeaway
Measured US data center use was about 176 TWh (4.4% of US electricity) in 2023 (LBNL, Dec 2024). Mainstream 2028–2030 projections range from about 6.7% to 17%, and the highest long-range views (BNEF, Dec 2025) reach about 20% by 2035. Every major forecaster has revised upward each year from 2022 through 2025. The spread between the low and high cases, roughly 2x, is itself the main sign of how uncertain these numbers are.

### Cited Findings
**Baselines and near-term (measured or short-term):**
- LBNL (Dec 2024): US data centers used 176 TWh in 2023, 4.4% of US electricity. AI servers alone went from 2 TWh (2017) to 40 TWh (2023). — [LBNL 2024 report](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/lbnl-2024-united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report_1.pdf); [Berkeley Lab news](https://bies.lbl.gov/news/berkeley-lab-report-evaluates-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers)
- LBNL (Dec 2024) 2028 scenarios: 325–580 TWh, or 6.7%–12.0% of forecast 2028 US consumption. The range comes from scenarios on GPU shipments, cooling and PUE. — [LBNL 2024 report](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/lbnl-2024-united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report_1.pdf)
- EIA STEO (Sept 2026): total US retail sales forecast at a record 4,135 BkWh in 2026 and 4,211 BkWh in 2027. Commercial sales, where most data centers are counted, grow 3.3% in 2026 and 2.7% in 2027. That is 63% and 56% of total sales growth. Commercial sales (1,550 BkWh) pass residential (1,508 BkWh) in 2026 for the first time on record. — [EIA STEO Sept 2026](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf); [APPA summary](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/eia-forecasts-us-will-use-record-amounts-electricity); [EnergyNow, Sept 2026](https://energynow.com/2026/09/us-power-use-to-beat-record-highs-in-2026-and-2027-as-ai-use-surges-eia-says-6/) **[snippet]**. Note: Utility Dive reported separately that EIA had *cut* its 2026 generation forecast by more than a percentage point in an earlier 2026 STEO, so EIA's near-term numbers have also moved downward at times. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/energy-short-term-outlook-2026-load-demand-data-centers/807530/) **[snippet, headline only]**
- Goldman Sachs (2026 article): US data center power demand of 31 GW in 2025, 41 GW in 2026 and 66 GW in 2027. Scheduled capacity additions are 13.6 GW (2026) and 36.3 GW (2027). Goldman assumes only about 60% of capacity scheduled for next year arrives on time, falling to about 50% two years out. — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/us-data-center-power-demand-projected-to-double-by-2027) **[snippet]**
- Gartner (June 10, 2026): global data center electricity rises about 26–27% in 2026, to 132 GW from 104 GW in 2025. This is a global figure, not US. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-10-gartner-says-data-center-electricity-demand-to-grow-26-percent-in-2026) **[snippet]**

**Medium/long-term projections:**
- IEA *Energy and AI* (April 2025), Base Case: US data center use rises about 130% (+~240 TWh) to about 426–430 TWh by 2030. Data centers drive nearly half of US electricity demand growth to 2030. Global data centers reach about 945 TWh (just under 3% of world electricity). The US and China together account for about 80% of global growth. — [IEA Energy demand from AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai); [AA summary](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/energy/electricity/ai-to-quadruple-data-center-electricity-demand-by-2030-iea/48526) **[snippet]**
- IEA *Electricity 2026* (early 2026): US data centers make up about 50% of US demand growth out to 2030. — [IEA Electricity 2026 – Demand](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/demand) **[snippet]**
- EPRI *Powering Intelligence* (2024; updated scenarios later, plus a "Powering Intelligence 2026" site): data centers reach 9%–17% of US electricity by 2030. Method: state-level tallies of operating capacity, capacity under construction, and announced plans. The low case counts most under-construction projects plus one-quarter of advanced-planning projects. The high case counts all under-construction and advanced-planning projects plus 30% of early-planning ones. — [EPRI press release](https://www.epri.com/about/media-resources/press-release/trb5wwt7oemdbkaamxrccqkq2ktteae8); [EPRI updated scenarios](https://restservice.epri.com/publicattachment/97025); [Powering Intelligence 2026](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/) **[snippet; I could not confirm the exact 2026-edition numbers]**
- BloombergNEF (Dec 2025): US data center demand reaches 106 GW by 2035, up from 78 GW in its prior outlook (the snippet calls the 78 GW a "2025" figure, which reads as the earlier forecast for the same horizon, not current load). Coverage reports that BNEF puts data centers at about 20% of US electricity by 2035, versus about 5.9% "today". — [Utility Dive/Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/u-data-center-power-demand-143033230.html); [KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bloombergnef-forecasts-us-data-centers-to-consume-20-of-electricity-by-2035) **[snippet; KuCoin is a weak secondary source]**
- Grid Strategies (Dec 22, 2025, *Forecasting for Large Loads*, and its national load growth report): utility forecasts add up to about 166 GW of new US peak demand by 2030. That is about a 20% increase over 2025 peak and roughly 6x the near-flat growth forecast in 2022. Data centers are about 55% of the growth, around 90 GW, or nearly 10% of forecast 2030 peak. — [Grid Strategies Forecasting for Large Loads](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/forecasting-for-large-loads/); [Grid Strategies 2025 report](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/wp-content/uploads/Grid-Strategies-National-Load-Growth-Report-2025.pdf); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/one-us-load-growth-estimate-quadrupled-in-just-two-years/) **[snippet]**
- NERC 2025 LTRA (released Jan 2026): 224 GW of summer peak demand growth by 2035, up 69% from 132 GW in the 2024 LTRA. Winter peak growth is 245 GW, up 65%. Data centers and AI are named as the main driver. — [NERC LTRA Jan 2026](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/our-work/assessments/nerc_ltra_2025.pdf); [POWER magazine](https://www.powermag.com/nerc-warns-long-term-grid-reliability-risks-mounting-from-surging-demand-lagging-resources/) **[snippet]**
- PJM 2026 Long-Term Load Forecast (Jan 2026): +32 GW of peak load from 2024 to 2030, 94% of it from data centers. Summer peak grows about 3.6% a year over the decade, against 0.3% a year in the 2021 forecast. Summer peak could rise about 85 GW over 15 years to more than 241 GW. PJM also trimmed its *near-term* outlook in 2026 while keeping the long-term trajectory. — [POWER: PJM dials back near-term](https://www.powermag.com/pjm-dials-back-near-term-load-outlook-but-maintains-steep-long-term-growth-trajectory/); [PJM 2025 load report](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/library/reports-notices/load-forecast/2025-load-report.pdf) **[snippet]**

**Methodology differences:**
- LBNL and IEA are bottom-up models of energy (TWh): server shipments × power × utilization × PUE. Utility, Grid Strategies, NERC and PJM figures are **peak-demand (GW)** forecasts built from customer requests and contracts. BNEF, EPRI and Goldman use project-pipeline tallies with assumed completion rates. GW of "capacity" and GW of average draw are not the same thing. Grid Strategies criticizes some utilities for using "unrealistically high load factors". — [Utility Dive: Grid Strategies VP on load factors](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/some-load-forecasts-using-unrealistically-high-load-factors-grid-strateg/805927/) **[snippet, headline]**

### Inferences
- **The GW figures conflict:** Goldman puts 2025 US data center demand at about 31 GW, while BNEF's figures for the same period are about 78 GW. The gap almost certainly comes from definitions (actual average draw versus capacity/pipeline). The report should not compare these directly.
- Converting the LBNL 2023 figure, 176 TWh is about 20 GW of average load. Mid-range projections of 65–90 GW added by 2030 would therefore mean roughly 4–5x growth in average data center load within about seven years.
- Every forecast series (NERC, Grid Strategies, PJM, BNEF) was revised up from 2022 to 2025 and into early 2026. PJM's 2026 trim to its near-term outlook and EIA's cut to its 2026 generation forecast are early signs of near-term moderation.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 update of the LBNL report turned up (the next one is mandated, but its status is unknown). I found no measured 2025 US data center TWh figure from a primary statistical source; EIA does not report data centers as a separate category.
- I could not confirm the exact numbers in EPRI's "Powering Intelligence 2026" or McKinsey's latest US forecast because the pages were blocked or not found.

## 2. Large-load interconnection queues and "phantom" load

### Takeaway
Large-load requests are an order of magnitude larger than what is being built. ERCOT's large-load queue grew to roughly 410–438 GW by mid-2026, while only about 9 GW was approved to energize and about 3.9 GW of observed peak large-load consumption was actually operating. Independent analysts (Grid Strategies, Wood Mackenzie) judge that utility forecasts overstate data center load by roughly 25–40%, and that most requested load (about 70%+) will never connect.

### Cited Findings
- ERCOT large-load queue: about 238.6 GW in March 2026. 198 GW applied in Q1 2026 alone. About 438 GW was being tracked by June 2026 (RTO Insider headline: "hits 410 GW"). About 87% of it is data centers. Only about 9.0 GW had Approval to Energize, and observed peak large-load consumption was about 3.9 GW, roughly a 1.6% request-to-operating ratio. Latitude Media reported the queue "nearly quadrupled in a single year". ERCOT is moving to a batch study process ("Batch Zero", PGRR145). — [ERCOT April 2026 Senate testimony](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/04/01/ERCOT_LargeLoad_Update_April2026_B-C_-Hearing.pdf); [ERCOT April 9, 2026 House testimony](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/04/09/ERCOTLargeLoadUpdate-April9HouseStateAffairsHearing.pdf); [ERCOT May 2026 board item](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/05/24/8-Interconnection-and-Grid-Analysis-Update.pdf); [RTO Insider](https://www.rtoinsider.com/129421-ercot-large-load-requests-soar-again/); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/ercots-large-load-queue-has-nearly-quadrupled-in-a-single-year/) **[snippet; the 410 and 438 GW figures differ by date or definition; check against ERCOT documents]**
- AEP (Q4 2025 earnings, early 2026): contracted large-load pipeline doubled to 56 GW by 2030, from 28 GW in Q3 2025. The Texas data center pipeline went from 13 GW to 36 GW. Data centers are about 90% of the pipeline. — [Utility Dive: AEP 56 GW](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/aep-large-load-data-center-earnings/812181/) **[snippet]**
- Dominion Energy: about 47 GW of data center capacity under contract (Sept 2025), rising to more than 48 GW by Dec 2025 (DCD called this a "marginal increase"). Five-year capex was raised 30% to $65B, with more than 90% of the increase in Dominion Energy Virginia. — [DCD: nearly doubles to 40GW](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/dominion-energy-nearly-doubles-data-center-capacity-under-contract-to-40gw/); [DCD: marginal increase](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/dominion-reports-marginal-increase-in-data-center-pipeline/) **[snippet]**
- Georgia Power (Nov 2025 filing to regulators): large-load pipeline of 50.9 GW, of which 11 GW across 28 projects is under contract. A separate economic development pipeline figure is 36.5 GW by the mid-2030s, 31 GW of it data centers. — [Latitude Media Q3 earnings](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/third-quarter-earnings-and-utilities-data-center-moment/); [Inside the Datacenter – Georgia](https://insidethedatacenter.com/articles/georgia-data-centers) **[snippet]**
- Grid Strategies (Dec 2025): aggregate utility forecasts probably overstate 2030 national demand by about 25 GW. Utility data center forecasts total about 90 GW by 2030, while market analysts' supply-chain-based estimates say growth is "unlikely to require much more than 65 GW". Utilities may be overstating data center demand by up to 40%. Causes cited are developers pitching the same project to several utilities and speculative proposals. — [Grid Strategies](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/forecasting-for-large-loads/); [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/data-centers/data-center-power-forecasts-climb-to-unreachable-heights) **[snippet]**
- Wood Mackenzie (2026; exact date unconfirmed): of about 1,066 GW requested for US data center projects, grid operators will commit to serving about 298 GW. About 768 GW, or about 72%, is "phantom" load. — [TFTC summary of WoodMac](https://www.tftc.io/ai-data-center-phantom-power-demand-wood-mackenzie); [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/technology/ai-impact-utility-forecasting/) **[snippet; secondary source; find the WoodMac original]**
- Industry experts estimate 5–10x more requests than actual builds. PJM called data center projections "unclear and uncertain" and pushed utilities to remove duplicate requests. Energy Secretary Chris Wright urged FERC to "deter speculative projects". — [LPPC/Reuters: 'Phantom' data centres](https://www.lppc.org/news/phantom-data-centres-muddy-forecasts-for-us-power-needs); [Utility Dive: a fraction will get built](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/a-fraction-of-proposed-data-centers-will-get-built-utilities-are-wising-up/748214/) **[snippet]**
- PJM capacity market effects: the Dec 2025 auction cost $16.4B. The market monitor (Monitoring Analytics) attributed $6.5B to data centers, $6.2B of it for facilities **not yet built**. The July 2026 auction for 2028/29 cleared 138,318 MW UCAP and fell 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement even counting FRR. That was the second consecutive shortfall. — [POWER magazine](https://www.powermag.com/pjm-widens-response-to-data-center-load-as-capacity-shortfalls-deepen/); [Avanza Energy Substack](https://avanzaenergy.substack.com/p/the-164-billion-reliability-tax-how) **[snippet; the Substack is secondary]**

### Inferences
- Adding up named utility pipelines gives more than 150 GW of "contracted" or pipeline load across just AEP (56), Dominion (48) and Georgia Power (11 contracted / 51 pipeline), plus a 400+ GW ERCOT queue. That is far more than the roughly 65–90 GW of data center growth most independent analysts expect by 2030, which confirms heavy duplication and speculation even among "contracted" figures.
- Phantom load costs real money before any of it is built. PJM's capacity prices already include billions for unbuilt data centers, so ratepayers pay for forecast error in either direction.

### Gaps
- Oncor-specific queue numbers (frequently cited as about 200 GW in its own large-load queue) were not confirmed in this pass.
- MISO and SPP large-load totals were not gathered.

## 3. Individual project scale and hyperscaler capex

Single campuses now sit at the 1–3+ GW scale, comparable to a large nuclear plant or a mid-sized city's peak load. Big-4 hyperscaler capex is guided at about $720–745B for 2026, up about 75% from about $410B in 2025. Some projects are slipping: OpenAI and Oracle dropped a 600 MW Abilene expansion in March 2026.

## 4. Supply-side constraints

### Takeaway
Supply is growing far more slowly than requested load. Heavy-duty gas turbines ordered now deliver around 2031. Large transformer lead times run 2–4 years, with about a 30% supply shortfall. High-voltage transmission construction is near historic lows (322 miles at 345 kV+ in 2024 versus a needed ~5,000 miles a year). Median generator interconnection takes more than 5 years. NERC flags 5 regions (MISO, PJM, ERCOT, WECC-Basin, WECC-NW) as high risk by 2030, and PJM's capacity auctions are already clearing short.

### Cited Findings
- **Gas turbines:** GE Vernova's gas backlog plus slot reservations reached 116 GW at the end of Q2 2026, up from 100 GW in Q1 2026 and 83 GW at the end of 2025. It targets at least 125 GW under contract by year-end 2026. Heavy-duty turbines ordered now arrive around 2031 (per the July 22, 2026 earnings call). Output is about 20 GW a year from Q3 2026, rising to 24 GW (2028) and a planned 30 GW (2030). About 20% of customers are data centers. Electrification booked $2.4B in data center orders in Q1 2026 alone, more than all of 2025. — [GE Vernova Q2 2026 press release (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000147/gevpressrelease2q26.htm); [GE Vernova Q1 2026 press release (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000063/gevpressrelease1q26.htm); [Utility Dive: backlog 116 GW](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-climbs-to-116-gw/826039/); [Turbomachinery Magazine](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-hits-116-gw-as-power-orders-more-than-double) **[snippet]**
- **Transformers:** Wood Mackenzie reports generator step-up (GSU) and substation transformer lead times above 160 weeks by Q1 2026, and a DOE-funded national lab put the largest units at up to 4 years (May 2026). WoodMac models a 30% supply shortfall for power transformers and 10% for distribution transformers in 2025–26, narrowing to about 5% by 2030 (GSU deficit going from 47% to 14%). GSU demand rose 274% and substation transformer demand 116% from 2019 to 2025. — [pv magazine USA, May 11 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/); [Industrial Sage](https://www.industrialsage.com/power-transformer-lead-times-us-grid-shortage/) **[snippet]**
- **Transmission:** only 322 miles of 345 kV+ lines were completed in 2024, the third slowest year in 15, against about 4,000 miles in 2013. DOE's 2024 National Transmission Planning Study calls for about 5,000 miles a year. — [ACEG/Grid Strategies "Fewer New Miles" 2025](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/wp-content/uploads/ACEG_Grid-Strategies_Fewer-New-Miles-2025_vF.pdf); [ACEG press release](https://cleanenergygrid.org/new-report-reveals-u-s-transmission-buildout-lagging-far-behind-national-needs/)
- **Generator interconnection queues (LBNL Queued Up):** the median time from request to commercial operation rose from under 2 years (2000–07 builds) to more than 4 years (2018–24) and more than 5 years for 2025 builds. At the end of 2024, about 10,300 projects (1,400 GW of generation plus about 890 GW of storage) were in queues, down 12% from the year before. The backlog "eased somewhat" in 2025. — [LBNL Queued Up 2025](https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/queued-2025-edition-characteristics); [APPA on 2025 easing](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/backlog-power-plants-seeking-transmission-grid-connection-eased-somewhat-2025-lbnl)
- **Reliability assessments:** NERC 2025 LTRA (Jan 2026) found 13 of 23 assessment areas at elevated or high resource-adequacy risk within 5 years. MISO, PJM, ERCOT, WECC-Basin and WECC-NW are at "high" risk by 2030 because new resources lag demand. Grid Strategies argued NERC *overstates* the risk. — [NERC LTRA](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/our-work/assessments/nerc_ltra_2025.pdf); [Utility Dive: Grid Strategies critique](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/nerc-overstates-reliability-risks-ltra-grid-strategies/814292/) **[snippet]**
- **PJM:** expects a 5.2% capacity shortfall by delivery year 2027/28, with about $15B in new plant investment needed. The July 2026 auction for 2028/29 was 6,831 MW short. — [POWER magazine](https://www.powermag.com/pjm-widens-response-to-data-center-load-as-capacity-shortfalls-deepen/) **[snippet]**
- Goldman Sachs: regional effects diverge. The Mid-Atlantic, Mid-Continent and Northwest face elevated reliability risk, while Texas and Georgia are relatively marginal thanks to planned generation. — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/us-data-center-power-demand-projected-to-double-by-2027) **[snippet]**

### Inferences
- GE Vernova's planned output of about 20–24 GW a year is *global*. Even if all of it went to the US, it would cover only a fraction of 65–90 GW of data center growth by 2030 plus other load and retirements. This is why on-site generation, aeroderivatives and reciprocating engines, and delayed coal retirements are spreading.
- On equipment and transmission lead times (4–5+ years), supply cannot respond inside the 2–3-year build cycle of a data center. The mismatch is about timing at least as much as about total quantity.

### Gaps
- I did not collect specific Siemens Energy or Mitsubishi Power backlog figures, or 2025–2026 data on scheduled versus delayed retirements (EIA retirement schedules, DOE 202(c) orders keeping coal plants open).
- I found no 2025 transmission-mile figure.

## 5. Skeptical views and historical overforecasting

### Takeaway
The skeptics' case rests on three points: (a) a documented history of IT energy forecasts that overshot badly; (b) duplicate and speculative requests in queues; and (c) physical supply limits (chips, turbines, transformers) plus financing risk that cap how fast load can actually arrive. Efficiency gains like DeepSeek's have so far been read through a Jevons-paradox lens and have not visibly reduced capex.

### Cited Findings
- Jonathan Koomey (ex-LBNL/Stanford) documented that some late-1990s and 2000s claims about IT electricity overshot by large factors. Data centers ended up at only "a couple percent" of US electricity by the late 2000s, against predictions of runaway growth. He argues forecasts routinely underestimate efficiency gains. He has published a guide for regulators, "Separating fact from fiction in data center electricity forecasts." — [Latitude Media: a skeptic's take](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/catalyst-a-skeptics-take-on-ai-electricity-load-growth/); [Koomey blog](https://www.koomey.com/koomey_blog/new-report-out-today-separating-fact-from-fiction-in-data-center-electricity-forecasts-a-guide-for-regulators/); [Koomey 2011, Growth in DC electricity use 2005–2010](https://alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/old/4363/Growth_in_Data_Center_Electricity_use_2005_to_2010.pdf) **[snippet; the "factor of 2000" claim in the search summary is unverified, so do not use it without checking]**

### Inferences
- The skeptic and bull cases are closer than they look. Even the "deflated" independent estimate (about 65 GW of new data center load by 2030) is roughly 3x the 2023 average load and matches or exceeds what turbine, transformer and transmission supply can deliver on that timeline. The real debate is whether forecasts of 90 GW or more (or queues of 400 GW or more) are credible, not whether growth is large.
- Unlike in the 2000s, today's growth is backed by contracted capex of more than $700B a year and measured sales increases (EIA's commercial sales overtaking residential), so the "phantom" problem is about the size of the overshoot, not about whether growth exists at all.
- The main downside risks are capital-market or AI-revenue disappointment (financing strain is already visible), chip supply limits, and efficiency gains that outrun demand. On a 2–3 year horizon, the supply chain caps how quickly load can arrive, which limits upside forecast error regardless of how many requests are in queues.
