# Counterarguments and Alternatives to a Federal Hyperscale Data Center Moratorium on Grid-Reliability Grounds (as of Oct 2, 2026)

Context note for the report writer: the main federal vehicle is the Sanders/Ocasio-Cortez "AI Data Center Moratorium Act" (S.4214, introduced Mar 25, 2026; House companion H.R.9442, Jun 24, 2026, nine cosponsors). It would halt building or upgrading data centers of 20 MW or more until Congress passes broad AI safeguards for workers, consumers, the environment and civil rights. So its trigger for lifting the halt is AI legislation, not a grid-reliability metric. That matters for the stress test: even if the grid case for a pause holds up, the bill's end condition does not depend on the grid at all. — [Axios, Mar 25 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/25/sanders-aoc-data-center-moratorium-bill); [Sanders Senate office](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/in-the-news/bernie-sanders-and-aoc-introduce-bill-to-pause-building-of-new-datacenters/); [Washington Post, Mar 25 2026](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/03/25/sanders-data-centers-bipartisan-moratorium/); [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/policymakers-consider-temporary-pause-on-ai-data-center-construction-what-stakeholders-need-to-know/). A separate House bill was filed by Rep. Carson ([Carson press release](https://carson.house.gov/media/press-releases/carson-introduces-data-center-moratorium-bill)); I did not review its details.

## 1. Load flexibility as an alternative to a pause

### Takeaway
There is a credible and fast-growing body of evidence that data centers can cut their demand during grid peaks. It includes the Duke headroom study, Google's 1 GW of demand-response contracts, Emerald AI's GPU-cluster demonstrations, EPRI DCFlex and mandatory curtailment under Texas SB 6. Together these let existing grids take on tens of GW of new load without new firm capacity. The main weakness is that the Duke numbers are system-level and theoretical, and critics say they are being misused as a substitute for firm interconnection.

### Cited Findings
- Duke Nicholas Institute, "Rethinking Load Growth" (Feb 2025): US balancing authorities could take on about 76–126 GW of new load if the new loads accept modest curtailment at peak. The 126 GW figure corresponds to curtailing about 1% of annual energy use. — [Duke report PDF](https://nicholasinstitute.duke.edu/sites/default/files/publications/rethinking-load-growth.pdf); [POWER Magazine summary](https://www.powermag.com/duke-researchers-grid-flexibility-key-to-accommodate-load-growth)
- "Curtailment-enabled headroom" means the extra load the existing system can absorb if the load curtails for relatively short periods. — [POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/duke-researchers-grid-flexibility-key-to-accommodate-load-growth)
- Counterpoint: an industry engineer argues the Duke study is being "misused" in the large-load interconnection debate. Advocates cite system-level headroom to argue for smaller network-upgrade obligations, or that VPPs can replace firm interconnection capacity. He says Duke's methodology and existing standards do not support either claim, because the headroom is not locational and ignores transmission constraints. — [Brian Handshy, Medium](https://medium.com/@brianwhandshy/dukes-rethinking-load-growth-study-is-being-misused-in-the-interconnection-debate-b7d3d981e8ca)
- Google announced 1 GW of data-center demand-response contracts with US utilities in March 2026. Counterparties include Indiana Michigan Power, TVA, Entergy Arkansas, Minnesota Power and DTE Energy. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/demand-response-data-center-milestone/); [Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/smart-grids/google-has-integrated-1-gw-of-data-center-demand-response-with-us-utilities/); [DCD on I&M/TVA](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/google-partners-with-im-and-tva-to-expand-use-of-demand-response-at-its-ai-data-centers/)
- Sep 16, 2026: Google, Nvidia and Emerald AI launched the AI Energy Management Alliance (AEMA), with Anthropic and RWE among the launch partners. Its goal is to change the rules so that facilities able to reliably cut demand get connected faster and may avoid some costly upgrades. — [Axios, Sep 16 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/16/tech-giants-launch-flexible-power-coalition-data-centers); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/google-nvidia-and-emerald-ai-found-the-ai-energy-management-alliance-to-support-demand-response-capabilities-within-the-data-center-sector/)
- Emerald AI demonstration: a 25% power reduction across a 256-GPU cluster for three hours during an Arizona grid stress event, while keeping compute service quality. EPRI DCFlex runs demonstration sites in Arizona, North Carolina, Illinois and France covering flexibility, power quality, backup systems and faster interconnection. — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/google-nvidia-and-emerald-ai-found-the-ai-energy-management-alliance-to-support-demand-response-capabilities-within-the-data-center-sector/); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/catalyst-the-mechanics-of-data-center-flexibility/)
- Skeptical framing: a commentary argues that "grid flexibility must be proven" at scale. — [remio.ai](https://www.remio.ai/post/emerald-ai-google-and-nvidia-launch-the-ai-energy-management-alliance-but-grid-f)
- Carbon Direct puts the value of enabling data-center load flexibility at about $5.5 billion. — [Carbon Direct](https://carbon-direct.com/insights/the-billion-dollar-case-for-enabling-data-center-load-flexibility)
- Texas SB 6 (2025) sets disclosure and curtailment obligations for loads of 75 MW or more. Co-located behind-the-meter loads must be able to curtail the full site within 30 minutes in a grid emergency, by physical breaker disconnection if needed, with 60 minutes' notice from ERCOT where practicable. These rules are final and being enforced. — [McGuireWoods, Jul 2025](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2025/7/texas-senate-bill-6-significantly-expands-regulatory-oversight-over-large-loads-in-ercot/); [Verse](https://verse.inc/blog/texas-sb6-curtailment-rules-ercot-data-centers); [White & Case on PUCT net-metering case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/puct-affirms-curtailment-authority-over-co-located-data-centers-first-net-metering)
- Brattle for GridLab/Utilize (Mar 2026): if a typical mid-size utility met 1 GW of new load with flexible data centers plus DERs, rates would be 4.8% lower than under a status-quo build-out of transmission, distribution and generation. — [Brattle "The Untapped Grid" PDF](https://www.brattle.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/The-Untapped-Grid-Mar-2026.pdf); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/grid-efficiency-could-save-utility-customers-more-than-110-billion/)

### Inferences
- A flexible-interconnection mandate (the Texas SB 6 model, or connect-and-manage) attacks the stated failure mode, peak-hour shortfall, more directly than a blanket construction halt, which also blocks flexible facilities.
- 1 GW of contracted demand response is real but small against forecast growth of hundreds of GW. Flexibility has been proven at pilot scale, not fleet scale.

### Gaps
- Duke's intermediate figure (reportedly about 98 GW at 0.5% curtailment) and its average curtailment-event durations were not re-verified from the PDF in this session; the PDF domain was not fetched.
- No independent, published performance data on Google's DR contracts actually being called.

## 2. Self-supply / "bring your own generation" (BYOG)

### Takeaway
Federal and RTO policy is moving to make data centers supply or pay for new generation. Examples: FERC's December 2025 PJM co-location order, PJM's BYOG fast track and Reliability Backstop Procurement, nuclear restarts (Palisades fuel loading in Aug 2026, Crane capacity rights approved in 2026), and on-site backup units that DOE has already pressed into emergency service. Each adds supply rather than freezing demand.

### Cited Findings
- FERC unanimous order, Dec 18, 2025: directs PJM to create three new transmission services for co-located load, clarify interconnection procedures for co-located projects, and reform Behind-the-Meter Generation rules, with compliance starting Jan 2026. — [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2025/december/ferc-issues-order-providing-guidance-for-co-locating-power-plants-with-data-centers-within-pjm); [Mintz, Feb 2026](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2151/2026-02-02-ferc-directs-pjm-issue-new-rules-co-location-power); [Day Pitney](https://www.daypitney.com/alert-federal-energy-regulatory-commission-orders-major-pjm-tariff-reforms-for-co-located-load-and-behind-the-meter-generation)
- PJM's December 2025 capacity auction fell 6,623 MW short of its reliability target. Data centers accounted for nearly 5,100 MW of the demand increase. — [Introl summary](https://introl.com/blog/ferc-pjm-colocation-ruling-data-center-power-plant-guide-2025) (secondary source; verify against PJM)
- January 2026: the White House National Energy Dominance Council and all 13 PJM-state governors issued a joint Statement of Principles. Data centers should bear the infrastructure costs of their own load growth, and an emergency backstop auction with 15-year terms was proposed. — [Ohio Capital Journal, Aug 10 2026](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/08/10/pjms-big-new-data-center-plan-make-the-states-figure-it-out/); [PJM Inside Lines](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-board-directs-action-on-resource-adequacy-affordability-and-large-loads/)
- PJM filed a one-time Reliability Backstop Procurement at FERC on Jul 31, 2026. It would run Sep 30–Oct 21, 2026, with results in early December and commitments of up to 15 years, before the 2029/30 capacity auction. It has two paths: bilateral matchmaking and a central backstop auction. Projects using "Bring Your Own New Generation" get expedited interconnection tracks. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-backstop-capacity-auction-ferc-data-centers/826792/); [PJM Inside Lines](https://insidelines.pjm.com/stakeholder-process-to-procure-generation-and-serve-large-loads-moves-forward/); [Utility Dive, earlier ~14.9 GW proposal](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-backstop-capacity-procurement-data-centers-ferc/817286/)
- The PJM Board has also asked FERC to approve curtailment of data centers ("connect and manage") amid capacity concerns. — [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/policy/news/55394729/pjm-board-seeks-ferc-approval-on-data-center-curtailments-amid-grid-capacity-concerns)
- Nuclear restarts: Palisades (Michigan) began fuel loading in August 2026 and entered Mode 6. — [ANS Nuclear Newswire, Aug 31 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-08-31/article-8355/fuel-loading-process-begins-at-palisades/). FERC approved Constellation's waiver moving PJM capacity interconnection rights to the Crane (former TMI-1) plant, as of June 2026. The Crane output is contracted to Microsoft. — search summary of [ANS, May 13 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-05-13/article-8026/ferc-decision-on-crane-restart-coming-in-june-or-july-constellation-execs-say/)
- DOE Emergency Order 202-26-23 (May 18–20, 2026) authorized PJM to run backup generation at data centers and other large loads as a last resort before or during an EEA-3 event, during an unusually hot mid-May. This shows on-site data-center generation being used as a grid resource rather than only a drain on it. — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/federal-power-act-section-202c-pjm-interconnection-llc-pjm-order-no-202-26-23); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/doe-emergency-order-signals-new-role-for-data-centers-backup-power/)

### Inferences
- The DOE emergency order cuts both ways. Data-center backup gen helped avoid load shed, but PJM reached emergency conditions in May, which moratorium advocates can cite as evidence of real stress.
- BYOG and backstop procurement mean new load arrives tied to new supply. A moratorium would also halt projects that bring their own capacity.

### Gaps
- Duane Arnold restart status, SMR deal timelines, and on-site gas-turbine totals were not researched in this session.
- FERC's ruling on PJM's July 31 backstop filing was not found (it may be pending).

## 3. Reliability policy tools short of a moratorium

### Takeaway
A dense toolkit now exists or is being built:
- FERC's large-load interconnection proceeding (show-cause orders to all six RTOs, Jun 18, 2026)
- NERC large-load standards (due to FERC by Dec 31, 2026)
- Large-load tariffs with minimum-take provisions, which shrank AEP Ohio's queue by more than half
- State pauses and audits targeted at speculative queue entries (Texas)

These address queue speculation, cost allocation and ride-through, which are the specific mechanisms of grid risk.

### Cited Findings
- DOE directed FERC (Oct 2025) to open a large-load interconnection rulemaking (RM26-4), preliminarily defining large loads as more than 20 MW. On Apr 16, 2026 FERC committed to act by end of June. On Jun 18, 2026 it issued Section 206 show-cause orders to PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE and NYISO. — [Holland & Knight, Apr 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/ferc-to-act-on-large-load-interconnection-docket-in-june); [Troutman](https://www.troutmanenergyreport.com/2026/04/ferc-commits-to-june-2026-action-in-large-load-interconnection-rulemaking/); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/ferc-large-load-interconnection-preliminary-rulemaking-key-takeaways-for-data-center-developers-other-large-load-projects-and-investors); [hData](https://blog.hdata.com/2026-ferc-rulemaking-large-loads)
- NERC is developing reliability requirements for large loads and expects to file them with FERC by Dec 31, 2026. — [CRS via EveryCRSReport](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49326.html). There are currently no voltage ride-through standards for large loads. Computational loads may switch to backup power instead of riding through faults, and that sudden loss of load can disturb frequency and voltage. — [NERC Large Loads FAQ](https://nerc.com/comm/RSTC/LLTF/Large%20Loads%20FAQs.pdf); [Texas RE, Aug 2026](https://texasre.org/pages/newsletters/august2026/largeloadrisk)
- AEP Ohio data center tariff (PUCO approved Jul 9, 2025): applies to new loads over 25 MW, with a minimum monthly billing demand of 85% of contract capacity. After it took effect, AEP Ohio's interconnection requests fell from 30 GW to 13 GW. PUCO also required AEP Ohio to end its de facto moratorium on new data center service. — [Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-business/policy-and-regulation/ohio-utility-regulators-approve-aeps-contested-data-center-tariff-proposal/); [Vorys](https://www.vorys.com/publication-public-utilities-commission-of-ohio-authorizes-tariff-for-aep-ohios-data-center-customers-requires-end-of-moratorium-on-new-services-for-data-centers); [AEP Ohio](https://www.aepohio.com/company/about/rates/data-center-tariff)
- Texas: on Aug 3, 2026 Gov. Abbott ordered the PUC and ERCOT to verify and audit all data-center projects in ERCOT's interconnection process before more advance, and to deny grid access to noncompliant projects. ERCOT missed the Aug 7 Batch Zero deadline and aims to finish the audit by December. ERCOT large-load requests total about 474 GW, more than five times its record peak; about 90% are data centers. — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/texas-gov-abbott-directs-data-center-audit); [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/august/texas-large-load-interconnection-update---ercot-batch-zero-pause-and-verification-process); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ercot-texas-puc-data-center-audit/828472/); [POWER/BNEF](https://www.powermag.com/texas-audit-could-delay-49-8-gw-of-data-center-load-cost-projects-up-to-15-billion-bnef-warns/)
- New York became the first state with a data-center moratorium on Jul 14, 2026: a pause of up to one year on state environmental permits while it builds a regulatory framework. — [Rockefeller Institute](https://www.rockinst.org/blog/updates-on-the-cloud-more-moratoriums-on-data-centers/)
- Industry alternative: OpenAI, Blackstone, SoftBank, QTS and major construction unions launched a multimillion-dollar coalition (late Sep 2026) pushing for stricter development standards instead of moratoriums. — [YourNews, Sep 30 2026](https://yournews.com/2026/09/30/7210062/ai-companies-private-equity-and-unions-launch-coalition-to-counter) (secondary; the full article could not be fetched)

### Inferences
- The AEP Ohio result suggests much of the "load" driving collapse projections is speculative. Financial-commitment tariffs remove phantom demand without stopping firm projects.
- The Texas action is a targeted, time-limited pause tied to verification, with an end date (about December 2026). It is a middle path between business-as-usual and a multi-year national halt, though it is still effectively a pause.

### Gaps
- Details of Dominion's GS-5 rate class (approved in Dominion's 2025 biennial review) were not retrieved.
- Outcomes of FERC's June 2026 show-cause orders (RTO responses, any final rule) were not found.

## 4. Harms a moratorium could cause

### Takeaway
The administration and GOP leaders oppose a moratorium mainly on China/AI-race and national-security grounds. Industry stresses investment loss. Quantified evidence of harm in this session comes from BNEF's estimate of the Texas pause alone (up to about $15B in delay costs and about 20% of the US pipeline affected). I found no rigorous quantitative estimate of offshoring, tax-revenue loss or stranded utility assets under a national moratorium.

### Cited Findings
- The White House opposes a moratorium or broad new rules on AI data centers, arguing it could let China pull ahead. Trump said Xi "will take every data center that we don't want." — [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6119096-trump-republicans-ai-data-centers/); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/media/mike-johnson-rejects-ai-moratorium-over-china-threat-conservative-activist-demands-guardrails)
- House Speaker Mike Johnson (Sep 2026) called opposition to data centers a "Chinese psyop." Republicans have opened inquiries into Chinese influence on local opposition. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/mike-johnson-rejects-ai-data-center-outrage-as-chinese-psyop-12497405); [Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/09/us-house-speaker-sees-ai-data-center-worries-as-chinese-psyop/); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/republicans-probe-chinas-influence-in-data-center-opposition/). Note: these are political claims, not evidence of grid effects.
- BNEF (Aug 5, 2026): the Texas pause could delay 49.8 GW of data-center demand, nearly 20% of the US pipeline. Delay costs are estimated at just over $8B by 1Q 2027 if 60% of delayed capacity is AI, and about $15B if it is all AI. BNEF warns the audit could run into the 2027 legislative session. — [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/texas-audit-could-delay-49-8-gw-of-data-center-load-cost-projects-up-to-15-billion-bnef-warns/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/bloomberg-nef-texas-grid-pause-puts-20-percent-of-us-data-center-pipeline-at-risk/); [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/texas-data-center-pause-puts-us-pipeline-risk-of-delay-bnef/827255/)
- Public opinion is moving the other way: 71% of Americans oppose a data center in their area (Embold Research), up from 42% the previous summer. — [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4200222/data-center-energy-constraints-and-moratoriums-are-mounting-expect-to-see-stalled-ai-projects.html)

### Inferences
- If the PJM backstop and utility build-outs are sized for data-center load, a federal halt could leave committed generation and transmission costs spread over fewer kWh. That is the stranded-cost and rate-increase mechanism, but I found no study quantifying it for a federal moratorium.
- A moratorium would remove the same high-load-factor revenue that the E3 study (Section 5) credits with downward rate pressure.

### Gaps
- No credible quantified estimate of AI compute offshoring, job and tax losses, or stranded utility investment under the federal bill. Industry-group (Data Center Coalition) figures were not retrieved.

## 5. Do data centers raise or lower residential rates?

### Takeaway
The evidence is mixed and turns on cost allocation. Historical LBNL/Brattle data link load growth to falling real prices, and the Amazon-commissioned E3 study finds data centers generating surplus revenue. Harvard ELI and UCS document real cost-shifting, especially in PJM. The policy implication favored by most of these sources is tariff and cost-allocation reform, not a halt.

### Cited Findings
- LBNL/Brattle (2025): historically, states with the highest load growth saw inflation-adjusted average prices decline. LBNL's newer analysis says whether new demand raises existing customers' prices depends on how costs are structured and allocated, not on the scale of the load. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/lbnl-retail-electricity-prices-rates-brattle/816321/); [Brattle Dec 2025 observations PDF](https://www.brattle.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Electric-Rates-and-Affordability-Observations-on-the-LBNL-Brattle-Study-and-Future-Trends.pdf); [Brattle 2026 update page](https://www.brattle.com/insights-events/publications/electricity-price-trends-2026-update-to-the-lawrence-berkeley-brattle-study/). The same LBNL/Brattle work expects retail prices to keep rising ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/lbnl-retail-electricity-prices-rates-brattle/816321/)).
- E3 for Amazon (Dec 2025): Amazon data centers produce about $33,500/MW of surplus revenue in 2025, rising to $60,650/MW by 2030. That is about $3.4M a year per 100 MW in 2025, roughly $6.1M by 2030. The mechanism is spreading fixed costs over more kWh. Utilities studied: PG&E, Dominion, Entergy, Umatilla Electric Co-op. Caveat: the study was commissioned by Amazon. — [E3 Ratepayer Study PDF](https://www.ethree.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/RatepayerStudy.pdf); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/some-amazon-data-centers-are-driving-down-utility-costs-amazon-commissioned-report-finds/); [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/amazon-data-centers-arent-raising-your-electric-bills-they-may-be-lowering-them/)
- Harvard ELI, "Extracting Profits from the Public" (Martin and Peskoe, Mar 2025): utilities profit from building infrastructure, so they have an incentive to offer Big Tech discounted special contracts and socialize the costs onto captive ratepayers through opaque ratemaking. — [Harvard ELI PDF](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Harvard-ELI-Extracting-Profits-from-the-Public.pdf); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/utilities-subsidize-data-center-growth-ratepayer-cost-shif/742001/)
- UCS (Mike Jacobs): $4.3B in 2024-approved transmission connection projects for data centers in seven PJM states, with more than 95% of 130 projects passing all costs to ratepayers. This is on top of an estimated $9.3B in data-center-driven capacity costs paid by PJM customers in Jun 2025–Jun 2026. — [UCS](https://www.ucs.org/about/news/billions-dollars-unreported-data-center-costs-pjm); [Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/pjm-data-center-transmission-costs-ratepayers/761579)
- Brattle "Untapped Grid" (Mar 2026): meeting 1 GW of new load with flexible data centers and DERs yields rates 4.8% below the status quo. — [Brattle PDF](https://www.brattle.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/The-Untapped-Grid-Mar-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest pro-moratorium evidence (UCS, Harvard ELI) concerns affordability and cost allocation, not grid collapse. It points to remedies like "large loads pay their own way," which the White House/governors principles and the AEP Ohio tariff already pursue.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-industry-funded) study found that rigorously shows data centers lowering residential rates in PJM specifically.
- Columbia CGEP's June 2026 load-growth commentary was found but could not be fetched (domain blocked).

## 6. Grid engineers' and analysts' views: regional and manageable, or collapse?

### Takeaway
Official reliability bodies describe elevated, regional resource-adequacy risk, which is serious in PJM, ERCOT, MISO and parts of the West and SERC. They do not describe nationwide "grid collapse." Independent analysts argue NERC's own assessment overstates even that regional risk by counting speculative and duplicate data-center load and ignoring flexibility requirements.

### Cited Findings
- NERC 2025 LTRA: 13 of 23 assessment areas face elevated or high adequacy risk over five years. High-risk areas are MISO, PJM, ERCOT, WECC-Northwest, WECC-Basin and SERC-Central. Ten-year summer peak forecast is up 224 GW, more than 69% above the prior year's forecast. In ERCOT and PJM, data centers could push margins below targets later in the decade. — [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/business/policy-and-regulation/nerc-warns-reliability-risk-is-rising-as-load-growth-outpaces-infrastructure/); [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/nerc-warns-long-term-grid-reliability-risks-mounting/); [Microgrid Knowledge](https://www.microgridknowledge.com/grid/article/55355943/nerc-warns-of-2028-capacity-shortfalls-amid-exploding-ai-and-data-center-loads)
- Grid Strategies (for Earthjustice, NRDC, Sierra Club, EDF) calls the 2025 LTRA "too pessimistic." It says the LTRA combines a high demand forecast with low assumed generation additions and low interregional flows. It does not account for data centers double-counted across multiple queues, supply-chain limits on data-center build, or new requirements for large-load flexibility and backup generation at peak. NERC defended its methodology. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/nerc-overstates-reliability-risks-ltra-grid-strategies/814292/); [Grid Strategies report PDF](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/wp-content/uploads/FINAL-2025-LTRA-Review.pdf)
- Queue inflation evidence: ERCOT's roughly 474 GW of large-load requests is more than five times its record peak ([POWER/BNEF](https://www.powermag.com/texas-audit-could-delay-49-8-gw-of-data-center-load-cost-projects-up-to-15-billion-bnef-warns/)). AEP Ohio's queue fell from 30 GW to 13 GW once financial commitments were required ([Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-business/policy-and-regulation/ohio-utility-regulators-approve-aeps-contested-data-center-tariff-proposal/)).
- Real technical risks are specific and solvable through standards: large-load ride-through and sudden load-loss events. NERC is writing standards for these. — [NERC Large Loads FAQ](https://nerc.com/comm/RSTC/LLTF/Large%20Loads%20FAQs.pdf); [Texas RE Aug 2026](https://texasre.org/pages/newsletters/august2026/largeloadrisk)
- CRS FAQ on data centers and the grid gives a neutral overview. — [EveryCRSReport R49326](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49326.html)

### Inferences
- "Collapse" overstates the official record. The risks are regional shortfalls under extreme weather, such as PJM's May 2026 emergency order, plus specific ride-through issues. Both have targeted tools: backstop procurement, curtailment mandates and NERC standards.
- A blanket national moratorium would also cover regions NERC does not rate high-risk, so it is a poor match for a regionally concentrated problem.
- Caveat for a fair stress test: real stress events have occurred (PJM's capacity shortfall of about 6.6 GW, DOE's May 2026 emergency order, Abbott's own reliability rationale for pausing Texas). The counterargument is about the remedy, not about whether stress exists.

### Gaps
- NERC's 2026 Summer Reliability Assessment and any 2026 LTRA were not reviewed.
- No direct quotes were gathered from individual grid engineers (for example RTO CEOs or FERC commissioners) on "collapse" framing.
